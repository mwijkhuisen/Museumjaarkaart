# Architecture: Muzelog (code name mjk)

Status: draft for the owner's approval (2026-11-01). Cited as A§x. Decisions are recorded in `docs/adr/`; this file is the overview. Facts marked **[U]** are unverified and are checked by the first phase that depends on them.

## 1. Product

- **What:** a public NL/EN website about Dutch museums: a catalogue of all ~1,360 museums in the Netherlands with search, filters and a map (A§7, A§15.3); a personal **museum passport** (visit log, progress against the Museumkaart list "37 van 512 · lijst nov 2026", wishlist, € saved with the card; A§10); ratings and reviews tied to a logged visit, with photos (A§11, A§12); public profiles. Later: helpful votes and comments, follows, a feed (P6), and video if beta users ask for it (P7).
- **Who:** anyone can browse. Sign-up is invite-only during the beta (D1) and opens in P5a. The audience is Museumkaart holders (1.54M cards, 9.7M visits in 2025 [U]) and museum-goers. Expect a niche: the closest trackers have tiny use (MuseoTrack ~85 monthly users, Cultra ~50 installs [U]).
- **The gap** (prior art, 2026-11-01): passports exist only as private, mostly iOS apps, and public reviews live on Google and Tripadvisor, which know nothing about the card. Nothing combines a web-first public passport with reviews by card holders tied to their visits (ADR-0001). Risks: A§20.
- **Brand:** **Muzelog**, domain `muzelog.nl` (owner, 2026-11-26; D5, `docs/plan/research/naming.md` §4). `muzelog.nl` is registered (2026-11-26); the trademark check by hand is still open in A1. The brand and domain never contain "Museum(jaar)kaart". It came from the shortlist Muselog / Muzelog, Museumvink, Museumlog. Museumlog has a high conflict: museumlog.app is "Museum Log", an international, English-language, pre-launch visit-logbook app (registered 2026-05-04), not a Dutch one.
  - Code reads the brand from config (`MJK_BRAND_NAME`, `MJK_DOMAIN`) and the i18n messages. The plan writes `<brand>` and `<domain>`. The code name `mjk` (D23) is never shown to visitors.
  - "Museumkaart geldig" appears only as a description. The footer says the site is not affiliated with the Museumvereniging or the Museumkaart (ADR-0006).
  - The public repository was renamed from Museumjaarkaart to `mjk` on 2026-11-26, before the first VM install (owner, 2026-11-22; A1): its old name carries the card's old name, which the Museumvereniging still treats as its brand.
- **Non-goals:** selling, renewing or showing the card; tickets; opening hours or an exhibitions agenda (we link to museum.nl and the museum's own site) · a native app (a PWA instead, P5a) · ads, affiliate links, donations or any other revenue (D14) · OpenStreetMap data in our database (only inside the basemap tiles) and Google data of any kind · scraping or copying museum.nl · social login (D8) · museum accounts or a right of reply in v1 (revisit after the beta).

## 2. Principles

1. **Small and boring.** One VM and one server image with roles. PostgreSQL does search, jobs and rate limits too. Add no service where a table will do (ADR-0002, ADR-0015).
2. **Isolated from Waterheight and F1.** Its own compose project, database, tunnel, networks, nftables table, secrets, GIDs, UID, subnets and timers. Nothing in `mjk` can break `rws` or `f1`, and nothing touches their paths (ADR-0003).
3. **Passport first.** The beta ships the catalogue, passport, wishlist, reviews, photos and profiles; the social layer comes after it (D18). Where the official side does something well (the card, the agenda), we link to it.
4. **Honest data.** Unknown stays unknown: `—` with the reason behind it. N names its source and list date. € saved names the museums without a price. Visits are self-declared, and the site says so.
5. **Privacy first.** No trackers, analytics, ads or third-party scripts and fonts. The browser talks only to `<domain>` and our own `media.<domain>`, and a Playwright test enforces it. One strictly necessary cookie. Photo metadata is stripped. Logs hold fixed codes, not people.
6. **Users' content is theirs.** Export at any time, deletion with a choice (D10), an upload licence that ends on deletion, and no sale or licensing of anything to third parties.
7. **Legal by design.** DSA notice and action, statements of reasons, the GDPR rights flows and the retention table are features with tests. They are built in M3, before the beta (ADR-0010).
8. **Never call external services from CI.** Wikidata, RCE, Lettermint, R2 and Cloudflare are recorded fixtures or stand-ins in tests. `record-fixture` refuses to run under `CI`.

## 5. Components

One server image (`ghcr.io/mwijkhuisen/mjk-server`) whose first argument picks the role (`node apps/server/src/main.ts <role>`), a backup image (`mjk-backup`: the `backup` target of `deploy/server/Dockerfile`, the server image plus `pg_dump` 18.6 and restic 0.19.1, so no other role carries them) and one web image (`mjk-web`: Caddy, the client assets, map glyphs, sprites and style). Compose reads the three refs from `MJK_SERVER_IMAGE`, `MJK_WEB_IMAGE` and `MJK_BACKUP_IMAGE`, and every image carries the label `org.opencontainers.image.revision`. Later `mjk-media` (ffmpeg worker, P7).

| Role | Kind | Does | DB login |
|---|---|---|---|
| `api` | daemon | Hono `:8080`, `/api/v1/*` JSON: auth, sessions, me/visits/wishlist/reviews, chunked uploads (writes `/srv/mjk/quarantine`), reports (DSA notices). `/api/admin/*` only on the admin host, with a Cloudflare Access JWT and a role | `mjk_api` |
| `ssr` | daemon | Hono `:8081`: renders public HTML (museum pages, lists, search results, public profiles, single reviews, legal pages) with React 19 from the Vite SSR bundle. | `admin <cmd>` | job | CLI, used until the admin UI exists. | `backup` | job | `pg_dump -Fc` + restic of the dumps and `/srv/mjk/originals` to the private R2 EU backup bucket (its own image `mjk-backup`, A§17.2) | `mjk_backup` (read-only) |

**Caddy routes** (web image):

- On `<domain>`: `/assets/*` (immutable, `file` matcher) · `/map/*` (glyphs, sprites, style) · `/api/v1/*` → `api:8080` · `/api/admin/*` → 404 · everything else → `ssr:8081`.
- On `admin.<domain>`: `/assets/*` (`file` matcher; `private, no-store` like every admin-host response, because A§15.6 wins over "immutable") · `/api/admin/*`, `/api/v1/auth/*` (except signup), `/api/v1/me*` and `GET /api/v1/museums*` → `api` · any other `/api/*` → 404 · everything else → the admin SPA entry (`admin.html`, same web build). Until P2a the admin host forwards only `/api/admin*` to the `api` (the auth, `me` and museums routes arrive with P2a), and "everything else" is a static placeholder page. The `api` checks the Access JWT on every admin-host request (A§9).
- Media and tiles never pass through Caddy: they come from `media.<domain>` (R2). The `api` also refuses `/api/admin/*` unless the `Host` is the admin host.
- Headers: the CSP (A§15.6), `Cache-Control` on `/api/*` as the `api` sets it per answer (A§14: short `s-maxage` on public GETs, `private, no-store` on everything personal, mutations and errors); Caddy sets `no-store` only on its own error responses, where `handle_errors` also repeats the A§15.6 headers (A§17.2). Public HTML with a short shared cache [U A§15.1], and `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` until P5b.

### 7.3 Matching

One matcher (`apps/server/src/museums/match.ts`, pure, property-tested) serves the sync, RCE rows, visit imports (A§10) and "report a change":

1. **QID** (directly, or through an RCE id already stored on a museum; a stored museum.nl URL counts only for imports and reports, because P973 on Wikidata is stale).
2. **Postcode + name:** the same 6-character postcode and a name similarity ≥ 0.4.
3. **Distance + name:** under 150 m (haversine, strictly) and a name similarity ≥ 0.5.
4. **Anything else** goes to the admin review queue. Nothing is merged automatically below rule 3.

Settled in P1a:

- **Names:**
  - The similarity is the best of the candidate's name and its aliases, after `normaliseName()`. That function lower-cases, folds accents (NFKD plus ß, ĳ, æ, œ, ø …) and turns punctuation into spaces.
  - It drops these whole words: `museum`, `musea`, `mus`, `het`, `de`, `t` (from `'t`), `van`, `der`, `den`, `en`, `in`, `stichting`, `stg`, `gem`. Place names stay. `mus`, `in`, `stg` and `gem` came from the RCE names' legal-form and abbreviation noise ("Markiezenhof, Gem. Bergen Op Zoom", "Mus Willet Holthuijzen"): on the full set they added one match and no ambiguous row.
- **`pg_trgm` in TypeScript:** `museums/trigram.ts` ports `similarity()` (shared ÷ union, as a float4; empty gives 0), so the matcher stays pure. It equals PostgreSQL 18 (builtin C.UTF-8) on 345 pairs (`trigram.int.test.ts`). The thresholds are compared as float4 too.
- **First rule decides:** the first rule with a passing candidate decides. One candidate is the match; two or more queue as `ambiguous`, and later rules are not tried.
- **Identity never changes by itself:**
  - A Wikidata row with an unknown QID that hits a museum by rule 2 or 3 queues, as `qid_conflict` when the museum has another QID (a merge or redirect) or `qid_link` when it has none.
  - An RCE row never takes a museum that already holds another RCE row.
  - Two new RCE rows on one museum both queue as `duplicate`.
- **Thresholds, calibrated on the owner's full recording** (O1, 2026-11-02: the pure seed plan on 1,344 live Wikidata items and 629 RCE rows; the [box] run re-checks them):
  - 492 RCE rows match (365 by rule 2, 127 by rule 3) and 137 queue: 115 with no candidate, 10 ambiguous, 12 duplicate (one museum, two RCE rows, such as a museum's second location).
  - The seed on the VM (2026-11-26, 1,336 live museums): 489 match (363 by rule 2, 126 by rule 3) and 140 queue (118 with no candidate, 10 ambiguous, 12 duplicate), within a few rows of the recording.
  - Of the 15 weakest matches (similarity 0.41 to 0.55), all but one or two are true pairs; raising rule 2 to 0.45 would lose about four of them to avoid one. So 0.4, 0.5 and 150 m stay.
  - Seven matches lie more than 500 m apart (the largest 64 km), and six of those are true pairs with a wrong or rough coordinate on one side, so rule 2 has no distance cap. Wikidata's coordinates win (A§7.2): the owner fixes such an item on Wikidata or pins the field.
  - The misses wait in the match queue: the same museum more than 150 m away (299 m and 187 m seen), and twin candidates at one postcode (two museums in one building).

### 7.4 Sync and diff

- **Weekly** worker job `museum_sync` (croner with `protect`, Tuesday 03:17 Europe/Amsterdam [U]; enqueued unless one is queued or running). The `sync-museums` role runs the same code. Runbook: `docs/runbooks/museum-sync.md`.
- **Seed** (owner, Q1): `sync-museums --seed`, allowed only while `museum` is empty.
  - It creates every Wikidata item that is not dissolved, and matches the RCE rows against those museums.
  - After the seed, a new museum queues. The weekly job refuses with `not_seeded` while `museum` is empty.
- **One run** (`museums/sync.ts`, as `mjk_worker`):
  1. A `pg_try_advisory_lock`; another run → `sync_busy`.
  2. Both sources fetched and parsed. A fetch error → `wikidata_unavailable` or `rce_unavailable`, retried.
  3. The empty guard.
  4. The state is loaded, the plan made (`museums/diff.ts`, pure) and applied in one `REPEATABLE READ` transaction.
  5. One log line of counts per outcome; never a payload, name or URL.
- **Dry run:** the same plan inside a `READ ONLY` transaction. It writes nothing, not even a run row, and prints a summary of counts and codes only.
- **Staging:**
  - Each row lands in `museum_source_row` with its payload (the parsed, normalised row), the sha256 of its canonical JSON, and the run that first saw or last changed it.
  - A row the owner or an earlier run has decided (matched or ignored) keeps its decision; only undecided rows are matched again.
  - An unchanged re-run writes nothing: every row keeps its `ctid` and `xmin` (`sync.int.test.ts`).
- **Auto-applied (safe, default deny; owner, Q4):** each writes an applied `museum_change`, so the history is complete.
  - website, street, house number, postcode, place, municipality
  - `name_en`, the descriptions, `museumnl_url`, `registered`
  - `rce_id` when an RCE row is matched
  - coordinates that appear or move under 250 m [U]
  - new aliases (aliases are never removed)
- **Review queue (unsafe):** everything else.
  - **The match queue:** source rows that are `new` or `ambiguous`, with a reason (`no_candidate` · `ambiguous` · `qid_conflict` · `qid_link` · `duplicate`) and candidates; `admin match list|resolve`.
  - **The change queue:** pending `museum_change` rows for a museum we hold:
    - a name change, a move of 250 m or more (one `coordinates` change), `categories`
    - a new or changed P18 (`commons_file`), a P576 closure (`closed_on`)
    - a safe field going to null
    - a row that disappears from its source (`presence`)
  - The change queue is worked with `admin change list|apply|reject`.
  - Nothing ever deletes: no login holds DELETE on a P1a table. Pinned fields are skipped and counted (`pinned`); the owner pins and unpins them with `admin museum pin <museum> <field…> [--unpin]` (any source-fed field but the QID; `lat` and `lon` together), which leaves an applied `pinned_fields` change.
- **Idempotent queue:**
  - The same pending news is not added twice.
  - Different news for the same field supersedes it (`superseded`), and so does a source that reverts.
  - A row that comes back clears its `presence` change.
  - While a museum waits for a presence decision (a matched row is gone and its `presence` change is pending, or it went this run), the sync changes none of its fields: otherwise the remaining source takes them over and swaps them back when the row returns (the real fixtures showed it, O1). After `--keep` the next sync updates it from the remaining source.
  - A change the owner rejected is not proposed again while the source repeats it (counted as `declined`); different news for that field queues as usual. To hold a value against every source change, the owner pins the field (`admin museum pin <museum> <field…> [--unpin]`).
- **Empty guard** (F1 pattern): the run applies nothing, records a `refused` run and fails the job with `empty_guard` (not retried) when, for either source:
  - its envelope fails the schema
  - more than 2 % [U] of its rows are invalid
  - it answers under 90 % [U] of the last *applied* run's rows (first run: under 1,000 Wikidata or 500 RCE rows [U])
- **Guard baseline:** it moves only with an applied run. After checking, the owner lets one run below it through for one source with `sync-museums --accept-drop <source>`.
- **Mass-change brake** (P1a review): a source can pass the guard and still be wrong. RCE's feature ids are contiguous and may be positional [U], and a decided row is kept by its id, so a reload that renumbers them would put a neighbour's address on every museum matched to RCE as safe changes. So a sync (never the seed) that would apply safe changes to more than 10 % [U] of the museums held, and to at least 50 [U] museums, applies nothing: it records a `refused` run with `mass_change` and fails the job without a retry, as the empty guard does. After checking, the owner lets one run through with `sync-museums --accept-changes`, recorded as `museum_sync_run.accept_changes`.
- Tests use recorded SPARQL and WFS fixtures (`scripts/record-fixture.ts`, which refuses under `CI`), plus hand-made `edge` and `hostile` cases, and `week2`, a week of news derived from `normal`.

### 7.5 Curation

  - The owner works through the `registered` museums first (503 on Wikidata, A§7.2): the card is valid at the Museumvereniging's members, and full membership needs the Museumregister [U].
  - Members use "Meld een wijziging" on a museum page; reports go to the moderation queue, and the owner applies them as `museum_change` rows.

- **N:** the number of museums in the last **published list version**. The owner publishes a version from the admin (`admin list publish [--date D]`; `mk_list_version` holds only the date). Publishing copies the museums then `participating` and not hidden into `mk_list_member`, in one transaction, and refuses an empty list, a second version on one date, a date after today (Europe/Amsterdam, `list_in_future`) and a date not after the newest version (`list_not_newest`), because a version is never removed. Changing a status afterwards changes neither N nor the version's members (`mk-list.int.test.ts`); no login can DELETE a list row. The site shows N with that date, "37 van 512 · lijst nov 2026", because public counts disagree (426 to 539). They also count different things: the Museumvereniging names 485 to 496 members with more than 500 locations (2026-11-22), and N counts our `museum` rows, one per location we hold.
- **Admission price:** the adult price in cents with `price_verified_at`. A museum without a price is left out of € saved, and the page says how many were left out. A price last verified more than 18 months ago [U] counts as no price (owner, 2026-11-22): prices change each January, so the owner re-checks them then (A6), and `admin museum export` lists the stale ones in a read-only `price_stale` column (P2c). The rule is judged against today, not the visit date (owner, P2c Q8; one price column, no history), by the Amsterdam date of `price_verified_at`.
- **Overrides (pinned fields):** the owner keeps a source-fed value against every later source change with `admin museum pin <museum> <field…>` and releases it with `--unpin` (P1a). Only the fields the sync writes after the seed can be pinned (`PINNABLE_FIELDS` in `museums/fields.ts`: every source-fed column but the QID; `lat` and `lon` together); anything else is refused (`not_pinnable`). Each change leaves an applied `museum_change` of field `pinned_fields` with both lists.
- **Card price:** a committed per-year table (2026: €75 adult, €39 under 19), updated by PR. Checked on the card's own site on 2026-11-22: €75 from 19 years, €39 up to and including 18, and €69 for a renewal; the table holds the new-card price (a renewal price can join later as a third value; owner, P2c Q9: `card_type` stays `adult` · `under_19`); no 2027 price is announced yet.
- **Photo policy:** curated from the museum's house rules and reports, shown before upload (A§13.3).
- **Closures:**
  - A museum closed on Wikidata (P576) or by the owner gets `closed_on`. Its page shows "Gesloten"; its reviews stay but become read-only.
  - Visits before `closed_on` can still be logged.
  - A temporary closure (owner, Q6): `temp_closed` and `temp_closed_until` (null = until further notice), set by the owner, with no free text, so it needs no translation or moderation.

## 8. Data model (PostgreSQL 18)

Times are `timestamptz` in UTC. Visit dates are `date` (local). Money is integer cents. - **They are `security_barrier`** (P1b review, owner decision 2026-11-02): they are the isolation boundary for `mjk_ssr`, which must see public rows only even when the `ssr` process is compromised and sends its own SQL.
  - **What passes the barrier.** Only leakproof quals pass below a barrier. The text, uuid, date and float8 comparisons of the list, the keyset, the province and municipality filters, the detail and near-me do, and still reach `museum_sort_idx`, `museum_slug_key`, `museum_pkey` and `museum_lat_lon_idx`.
    - The list's ORDER BY becomes a top-N sort above the barrier, ~4 ms at 1,400 rows.
    - The category filter (`@>`) is not leakproof and runs above it.
    - `@@`, `<%` and functions such as `mjk_unaccent` are not leakproof either.
  - **What it stops:** the error and value channel. `views.int.test.ts` proves, as `mjk_ssr`, that a qual that raises only for a hidden value never raises through any view, and that it does leak a hidden alias and a hidden old slug once the barrier is off.
  - **What it does not stop:** plan instrumentation (re-review of P1b, N1).
    - `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE)` needs only SELECT. A leakproof qual runs below the barrier on its index, so `Rows Removed by Filter` counts the hidden rows that `NOT hidden` dropped there.
    - That confirms a guessed hidden slug, id or coordinate, and range counts on the sort key can rebuild hidden names.
    - `views.int.test.ts` pins this residual.
  - **Later views.** Later `pub_*` views (`pub_media`, …) are barrier views too. Where hidden content is sensitive (removed, pending or DSA-removed), P3b decided (a) for `pub_media`:
    - (a) keep hidden rows out of the indexes a caller's leakproof qual can reach: `media_review_approved_idx (review_id)` and `media_museum_approved_idx (museum_id, id)` are partial `WHERE state = 'approved'`, which the view's own predicate implies, and there is no other index on those columns; the server's own indexes are on `user_id` (a column the view does not expose) or partial on states it excludes (`uploading`, unattached). The residual is the primary key (a guessed uuidv7 of a pending photo), accepted like `pub_museum`'s;
    - (b) for what (a) cannot reach, the existence and count channel is accepted, as P1b accepted it for the catalogue (P3b security review L1, code review L9). Only a compromised `ssr` or an injection could run `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE)`, and it learns a count, never content:

Ids are uuidv7, so that is how many photos were not approved per time window, and a guessed photo id is confirmable;
      - `pub_media`'s museum: `media_museum_approved_idx (museum_id, id)` holds only approved photos, but an approved photo of a removed review or a deleted author is in it, and the view's joins drop it above the scan, so a public `museum_id` shows how many such photos the museum has (`grants.int.test.ts` pins the `review_id` case alone). P3c may mark a removed review's photos `removed` instead;
      - `pub_museum_image`: `museum_image`'s key is `museum_id`, so a guessed museum id shows whether that museum has a `hidden` or `skipped` image row; a hidden museum's id is confirmable as through `pub_museum`.

## 9. Accounts and authentication

Own code following Lucia's `auth_session` approach (ADR-0007). No Google or Apple. Email is verified before any public content.

- **Sign-up (beta):** invite code, email, handle, display name, a 16+ confirmation, the current terms version, an ALTCHA solution and a honeypot field.
  - The `api` checks the rate limits, the ALTCHA (single use), the invite and the handle, then mails a code. The answer is the same whether or not the email already has an account.
  - `app_user` is created only when the code is entered (`email_verified_at`, `age_confirmed_at`, `terms_*` set), and the session starts. Open sign-up (P5a) drops only the invite.
  - Invite codes are 16 characters of Crockford base32 (80 bits), with `max_uses` 1 and `expires_at` 14 days by default (`admin invite create --uses N --days D [--email <address>]`). `uses` is incremented with `UPDATE … WHERE uses < max_uses AND expires_at > now()` in the transaction that creates the account; a used-up or expired invite makes the code verify fail with 403 and creates nothing.
- **Sign-in by email code:** email + ALTCHA → a 6-digit code by mail (codes, never links: mail scanners open links). Valid 10 min, 5 attempts, single use. Stored as an HMAC with a pepper from `app_keys`.
  - Issuing a code voids every unused code for the same email and purpose. Verify compares only with the newest code and names its purpose (`signin` · `signup` · `email_change` · `delete`); a code of another purpose fails like a wrong one.
  - `/auth/code` sends nothing for an email without an account (purposes `signin`, `delete`); the 202 and its timing are the same.
  - The `api` enqueues a `mail_code` job. Its payload holds the `email_code` id plus the code sealed with AES-256-GCM under the `code_seal` key from `app_keys`; sealed fields are the one exception to ids-only payloads (A§8). The worker opens it and sends the mail; a sent mail's job row is deleted in the sending step (it cannot share a transaction with the HTTP call). An unsent code's sealed field is nulled by the purge on its 10-minute tick after `expires_at`, so within about 20 minutes of issue; a dropped job is deleted at once. Mail jobs are never kept 30 days. `mail_invite` (from `admin invite create --email <address>` or `POST /api/admin/invites` with `email`) seals the invite code the same way. `mail_invite`, the notice to the old address on an email change, the notice of an added passkey (P2b; `mail_notice` with `notice: passkey_added`, its address sealed with that name in the associated data, so it cannot be replayed as the other notice) and the deletion confirmation (A§13.5) carry their recipient address sealed under `code_seal` next to the ids, gone with the job once sent (a failed job's sealed fields are nulled by the next purge); the address is never stored on `invite` or kept after the send. A code mail holds the code and fixed text only: no handle, display name or other submitted text.
- **Sign-in by password** (optional, set in settings): argon2id via `node:crypto` `argon2()` at no less than OWASP's floor (m = 19 MiB, t = 2, p = 1), with the highest measured m ≤ 64 MiB (19 MiB, then 24 to 64 in 8 MiB steps) that keeps one hash and two at once both under 250 ms on the VM: m = 56 MiB (measured on the VM, 2026-11-27; `docs/runbooks/accounts.md` B1); at most 2 hashes at once per process (a semaphore; a waiter gives up after 2 s with 429); passwords of 12 to 256 characters (else 400); no HIBP check (a third party). The PHC string carries the parameters, and a hash with older ones is redone at the next successful sign-in. One error for an unknown email and a wrong password, and the server hashes a dummy so the timing matches.
- **Sign-in by passkey** (P2b, as built 2026-11-27; `apps/server/src/auth/webauthn.ts`, @simplewebauthn/server 14.0.3 and @simplewebauthn/browser 14.0.0): discoverable credentials (`residentKey: 'required'`, a registration whose `credProps.rk` is false refused) and the browser's autofill UI; RP ID `<domain>` on both hosts, always passed; `expectedOrigin` is the one origin of the host the request arrived on (`https://<domain>` or `https://admin.<domain>`), never a list; `userVerification: 'required'` at registration and sign-in, and the server rejects a response without the UV flag; attestation `none` (owner, Q3); EdDSA, ES256 and RS256 only (passed explicitly, so the library never offers or probes ML-DSA). An email code always remains a way in: no passkey-only accounts, no second factor.
  - **Challenges are stateless** (owner, 2026-11-26, Q-A; P2a's ALTCHA pattern): 54 bytes, version ‖ kind (sign-in or registration) ‖ expiry ‖ a 16-byte nonce ‖ HMAC(`altcha_hmac`, 'webauthn', version, host, kind, expiry ‖ nonce, binding) (the version inside the MAC since the P2b security review), the binding empty for a sign-in and (user id, session ref) for a registration. So a challenge works only on its host, for its ceremony and, when registering, in the session that asked for it; 5 minutes; options write nothing. A challenge is spent once into `auth_challenge` (purpose `webauthn`, sha256 of its bytes, unique) by an `INSERT … SELECT … WHERE expiry > statement_timestamp() ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, with the database's clock (the purge's), **after** the signature verified and inside the transaction that acts on it: @simplewebauthn/server 14.0.3 awaits `expectedChallenge` before its origin, RP-ID, UV and signature checks, so a callback that consumed would let any forged response burn a challenge. Two verifies of one assertion serialise on the unique key; one wins. The expiry is checked with the statement's own clock, never `now()` (the transaction's start), and the purge deletes an `auth_challenge` row only a minute past its expiry, so a replay that starts just before a purge never finds the spent row gone (P2b security review L4).
  - **The signature counter:** the library's rule (`(new > 0 or stored > 0) and new ≤ stored` is refused) runs before the signature check, on unauthenticated data, so it gets `counter: 0` and we apply the same rule after `verified`: an equal or lower non-zero counter (or 0 after counting) is a 401 and the fixed warning `passkey_counter`, which only a key holder (a clone) can cause. The update of `sign_count` is a compare-and-set on the value checked, so a concurrent use or a deletion since the read refuses too. A passkey is not disabled by a counter refusal.
  - **Sign-in** (`POST /auth/passkey/verify`): the credential id finds one passkey and its user; an unknown credential, a deleted account and, on the admin host, an account whose email is not the Access JWT's get the same 401 before any signature work (so an unknown id answers sooner: accepted, credential ids are unguessable, A§16.2); the assertion's `userHandle` must be that account's `webauthn_user_id`; `crossOrigin: true` or a `topOrigin` is refused (the library lets a cross-origin response without `topOrigin` through); a banned account gets 403 `banned` only after its signature verified (as for a password); success is P2a's `startSession` with `method = passkey` (a new token, the sent cookie's row deleted, `reauth_at` now). Registration (`POST /me/passkeys`) first takes only attestation `none` with an empty statement or `packed` self attestation (`alg` and `sig`, no `x5c`), decoded with the library's own CBOR helper before the library sees it, and refuses anything else with the reason `attestation_format` (P2b security review H1: every other format makes the library walk an X.509 chain the client chose and fetch its CRL URLs, A§16.2 SSRF; with conveyance `none` a browser sends `none` anyway, WebAuthn L3 §5.1.3); it then checks the bound challenge, origin, RP ID (the library skips it when none is given), UV, `crossOrigin` and `rk`, takes the credential id from the authenticator data, and stores a credential id once (23505 → 409), at most 5 a day per member (`passkey_add_user_day`, the table below). A stored passkey also queues, in the same transaction, the notice of an added passkey to the account's address (owner, 2026-11-27; `mail_notice` with `notice: passkey_added`, fixed text, no passkey name, no device data, no link).
  - **The WebAuthn user** (owner, Q2): `user.name` and `user.displayName` are the handle, never the email; `user.id` is `app_user.webauthn_user_id`.
  - **Adding a passkey** (owner, Q1 and Q1a): `POST /me/passkeys/options` needs a sign-in or code within the last **5** minutes (10 minus the challenge's 5), `POST /me/passkeys` within 10; so a challenge that is still valid always ends inside the 10-minute window, and the phone never stores a credential the server then refuses. Removing needs 10 minutes and ends every other session.
  - **Re-authentication with a passkey** (owner, 2026-11-27; `POST /auth/passkey/reauth/options` · `/auth/passkey/reauth/verify`, member only): the "confirm it's you" panel (`Reauth`) offers one of the member's own passkeys beside the code and the password. It never switches accounts: the options list only the signed-in member's passkeys (`allowCredentials`, 1 to 20; none → 409), so the browser offers no other account's; the challenge has its own kind (`reauth`) bound to this member and session; verify refuses another member's credential (`other_member`) before any signature work, accepts a missing `userHandle` (the browser had the list) but checks one that is there, and otherwise runs the sign-in path (UV, origin, RP ID, `crossOrigin`, the counter rule, the spend, `passkey_fail_prefix`, `passkey_user_hour`). Success is a new session for the same member with `reauth_at` now and the current row deleted, as after a code (no login can UPDATE `reauth_at`). The sign-in page's autofill is never armed in `Reauth`.
  - Every refusal logs `passkey_refused` or `passkey_register_refused` with a fixed `reason` (`unknown`, `user_handle`, `challenge`, `verify`, `cross_origin`, `replay`, `changed`, `not_discoverable`, `id_mismatch`, `attestation_format`); never a credential id, key, challenge, user handle or email (`log-capture.int.test.ts`).
- **Sessions:** a 32-byte random token; the row id is sha256(token). Cookie `__Host-mjk_sid; HttpOnly; Secure; SameSite=Lax; Path=/` plus `Max-Age` (the absolute lifetime, so a phone keeps the session after the browser closes; P2a, owner 2026-11-26): host-only, so it never reaches `media.<domain>` and the public cookie never works on `admin.<domain>`. 30-day sliding expiry (as built in P2a: a request whose session was last used over an hour ago moves `last_used_at` to now and `expires_at` to now + the idle lifetime, never past the absolute one; at most one write an hour per session). Sign-out deletes the row; "sign out everywhere" deletes them all. Absolute lifetime 180 days [U]; admin-host sessions 12 h idle, 7 days absolute [U]. Signing in deletes the row of any session cookie sent with the request and always issues a new token.
- **Admin host (D22):** the `api` verifies the Cloudflare Access JWT (like F1's A§10) first on every request whose `Host` is `admin.<domain>`, the auth routes included. That test fails closed: any `Host` whose name part equals `admin.<domain>` gets the check, because Caddy's host matcher routes every such spelling to the admin block (P1e security review S6). The name part is what lies inside a leading `[` up to the first `]`, or else everything before the first `:` (an empty, odd or out-of-range port too) less one trailing `]`; then trailing dots and case are dropped; an admin-host session is created only after that check and only for an email equal to the JWT's. `/api/admin/*` also needs that admin-host session, a user with role `moderator` or `admin`, and the JWT's email equal to the user's. Built in P1e (`apps/server/src/api/middleware/access-jwt.ts`, ADR-0004): the JWT is the bare value of `Cf-Access-Jwt-Assertion` (hono's `hono/jwk` accepts only `Bearer`, so our own middleware runs on `verifyWithJwks`); RS256 with a `kid` only; `iss` is `https://<team>.cloudflareaccess.com`; `aud` contains `CF_ACCESS_AUD` (64 lowercase hex); `exp` required (hono's `verify` skips an absent one); `nbf` with 60 s of skew; `iat` unchecked; an `email` claim required. The certs come from `https://<team>.cloudflareaccess.com/cdn-cgi/access/certs`: fresh for 5 min, used stale for up to 1 h while a refresh fails, then a 503; an unknown `kid` is refetched at most once per 60 s. The fetch uses global `fetch` with a 5 s timeout, `redirect: 'error'` and a 64 KiB cap (no undici Agent, no new dependency). Every refusal is the same 401 with the log code `access_refused` and a fixed reason. The role check is P2a's gate in `apps/server/src/api/admin.ts` (the typed hook `AdminAuthorizer` it replaced is gone): no admin-host session → 401; a session whose user's email differs from the JWT's, or a role other than `moderator` or `admin` → 403; `GET /api/admin/me` answers handle and role. `readApiConfig` requires `MJK_DOMAIN`, `CF_ACCESS_TEAM` and `CF_ACCESS_AUD` (exit 78); the tests use `test-team` and 64 `a`s.
- **CSRF:** `SameSite=Lax`, our own Origin/`Sec-Fetch-Site` middleware on every unsafe method (A§14), and JSON-only mutations (anything else gets 415). Upload chunks are `PUT`, which is never a simple request, and the API sends no CORS headers.
- **Bot protection:** ALTCHA (`altcha-lib` 2.8.0 server, `altcha` 3.5.0 widget from `/dist/external`, so `worker-src 'self'` is enough) on sign-up and code requests, a honeypot field, the Postgres limiter, and one Cloudflare rate-limiting rule on `/api/v1/auth/*` (A2). On the Free plan that rule matches the path only (no host or method), counts per IP address over a 10 s window and blocks for 10 s (Cloudflare's rate-limiting docs, 2026-11-22): it damps a burst and nothing more, so the Postgres limiter is the control.
- **Client IP:** Caddy trusts only cloudflared's fixed /32, takes the right-most untrusted `X-Forwarded-For` address (Waterheight ADR-0021) and passes it to the `api` as `X-Mjk-Client` (A§17.2). The `api` reads `X-Mjk-Client` only when the TCP peer is `10.63.2.10` (caddy), otherwise it uses the peer's own address. From caddy, a missing or invalid header leaves the client unset (fail closed), never caddy's own address, which would put every such request on one limiter key (P1e security review S17). The address and its prefixes (/24, /56, /48) live on the request context only and are never logged; without a peer nothing is set, and P2a's limiter must then refuse (`apps/server/src/api/middleware/client-address.ts`). The limiter keys on the /24 (IPv4) prefix, or the /56 (IPv6) prefix and its /48 at 4× the limit [U].

- **Roles and bans:** `user` · `moderator` · `admin`. The `admin user role` CLI sets the first admin. A ban sets `status`, `banned_until` and `ban_reason`, revokes every session, stops new content and sends a statement of reasons (A§13.1). Existing content stays unless a moderator removes it, with its own statement.
- **Account settings:** email change (code to the new address, a notice to the old one), password set, change or remove, passkeys, the session list, visibility and display settings (A§10), export (A§13.2) and deletion (A§13.5, after a fresh code).
  - Email change, password set/change/remove, passkey add/remove and "sign out other sessions" need a sign-in or code within the last 10 min (`session.reauth_at`), else 403 `reauth_required`; a passkey sign-in counts. The start of a passkey add (`POST /me/passkeys/options`) needs 5 minutes (P2b Q1a, above).
  - Email changes, password changes and passkey removals delete every other session.
  - Lost mailbox: a password or passkey still signs in and can change the email; without either there is no recovery, and the owner never moves an account on request. This is stated on `/privacy` and in the sign-in help.
- **What is stored:** email, handle, display name, the password hash if set, passkey public keys, session hashes, code HMACs (24 h), the invite used, the 16+ confirmation time and terms version, IP prefixes in `rate_limit` windows, and the full IP of content-creating requests for 30 days [U]. No real name, birth date, phone number or tracking data.
- **As built in P2a** (2026-11-26; the phase plan's designs 1–12):
  - **The worker decides whether a code mail goes out.** `/auth/signup`, `/auth/code` and `/me/email` do the same work for every email after validation: they void the older codes, insert `email_code` and enqueue `mail_code` with the code sealed, in one transaction. The worker drops the job and the code row for a sign-in to an email without an active account, a sign-up for an email that has one, and an email change to an address that has one. So the 202 and its timing never depend on an account existing.
  - **Codes:** HMAC(otp_pepper, id ‖ code). Verify takes the newest code of the email and purpose and counts the attempt in its own conditional `UPDATE … attempts < 5 … RETURNING` before a constant-time compare; an email without a code runs the same statements against the nil id. A wrong, expired, used or exhausted code is one 401; a unique violation at sign-up is a 409. `mail_code` jobs have one attempt (a code lives 10 minutes, the queue's backoff starts at 15). `/auth/code` takes only `signin` until P3d adds `delete`.
  - **ALTCHA** (altcha-lib 2.8.0's v2 protocol, widget 3.5.0 `altcha/external`): SHA-256 only (the widget's SHA worker is Web Crypto; its argon2id and scrypt workers would need WebAssembly, which the CSP forbids); issued statelessly and signed with HMAC(altcha_hmac, host), so a challenge from one host fails on the other; spent once into `auth_challenge` by its signature's hash; 5 minutes; difficulty a 12-bit prefix with cost 1 [U P4 tunes it]. The widget's attribute is `challenge`, `hideFooter`/`hideLogo` go through `configuration`, its strings come from our messages through `$altcha.i18n`, and its payload is base64 of `{challenge: {parameters, signature}, solution}`.
  - **The limiter** (`apps/server/src/auth/limiter.ts`): the table above as buckets; one bucket `code_prefix` for code requests, sign-ups and email changes per IP prefix; password sign-ins per email keyed by the email's HMAC (the same key as the user for a real account, and it also covers an unknown email); every per-email key (`code_email_prefix`, `code_email_day`, `password_email`) also carries the host class, so a public-host flood never fills the admin host's buckets, and sign-up counts them only after a usable invite (P2a review); `PUT /me/password` per member (`password_set`, keyed by the member's HMAC like the passkey buckets) before the hash, so a signed-in member cannot keep both gate permits busy (P2a box review); failure buckets reserved before the compare and refunded on success (the exact window row), so parallel guesses cannot pass a limit; above 500 failures an hour site-wide, verify needs an ALTCHA solution and otherwise answers 400 `fields: ["altcha"]`, never a 429 that would lock members out (`/me/email/verify` answers the same 400 and takes no ALTCHA: the member retries later). No client address → 429 `client_unknown`.
  - **Account status:** only an `active` account signs in; `banned` gets 403 `banned` after the credential checks, with no session; `deleted` gets the unknown email's answer. The session middleware checks the status on every request. How a passed `banned_until` lifts a ban is P3c's [U P3c].
  - **Passwords:** argon2id through Node 26's `crypto.argon2` (async; it matches RFC 9106's argon2id vector, so the PHC string's `v=19` is right; the PHC string is ours, standard base64 without padding, parsed strictly with memory capped at 64 MiB). Every failed sign-in runs exactly one verify through the 2-permit gate (a dummy for an unknown email, an account without a password, an unparseable hash, a deleted account and an admin-host email other than the JWT's). The upgrade at sign-in is guarded by the hash just verified. The parameters are m = 56 MiB, t = 2, p = 1 (B1 on the VM, 2026-11-27: one hash about 70–90 ms, two at once about 190–200 ms, the medians; 64 MiB's pair median passed 250 ms in two of three runs). `dummyVerify` runs at the current parameters. m went up before the production database held any account (no code mail could be sent before A4), so no stored hash has the old m and the dummy needs no second parameter set, given that this image is deployed before the first password is set (`docs/runbooks/accounts.md` §2, with a count of stored hashes at other parameters that must be 0). A later change of the parameters must not just add a dummy at the old ones on the unknown path: that leaves three timing classes (known-old, known-new, unknown). While hashes of more than one parameter set are stored, every failed path costs one hash per stored set: a stored hash, its verify at its own set plus a dummy at each other set; an unknown email or an account without a password, a dummy at every set. That PR pins it with an integration test that stores an old-set hash next to a current one, shaped like the timing-median test in `apps/server/test/auth/password.int.test.ts`. The alternative is to rewrap the old hashes offline under the new parameters (argon2 at the new set over the old tag), so only one set is stored.
  - **The session middleware** reads the cookie only on `/api/v1/auth/*`, `/api/v1/me*` and `/api/admin/*`. On the admin host a session counts only while its user's email equals the Access JWT's (else none, flagged for the admin gate's 403), so a sign-in there still replaces a stale row.
  - **The anonymous mail budget** (`signup_site_day`, A§17.7) counts accepted sign-up requests, not sent mails, so it never tells whether an email has an account (P2a Q6, owner 2026-11-26). The month's sent mails count in `mail_sent`.
  - **`content_ip_log`** is written at sign-up and at a display-name change (Q7); `PATCH /me` with a new display name and no known client is refused (429), so the log stays complete.

## 10. Passport, wishlist, profiles and privacy

- **Visit:** user, museum, `visited_on`, a private note, source (`manual` · `bulk` · `import`). Several per museum, at most one per date and at most one undated. No future dates. `visited_on` may be null ("eerder, datum onbekend"), for onboarding and imports without dates.
- **Progress:** **card list** = distinct visited museums in the last published version's `mk_list_member`, against N, in total and per province ("37 van 512 · lijst nov 2026", A§7.5); **all time** = every museum ever visited, closed ones included; **per year** = distinct museums visited in a calendar year (dated visits only).
- **€ saved per calendar year:** Σ adult admission of the year's dated visits to museums that were `participating` on the visit date (`mk_since` null or ≤ date, and `mk_until` null or ≥ date), minus that year's card price for the user's card type. Museums without a price are left out and counted ("3 musea zonder prijs"). The adult price is used for everyone, which overstates the savings of under-19 holders; the page says so. A stale price counts as no price (A§7.5). A card runs 365 days from its activation, not a calendar year (the card's terms, 2026-11-22), so the calendar-year figure is an estimate, and the page says that too.
- **Wishlist:** user and museum. Filters "op mijn verlanglijst" and "nog niet bezocht" (A§14).
- **Onboarding:** after sign-up, "Welke musea heb je al bezocht?": search and province filters, tick many at once, then optionally add dates. The ticks become visits with no date; a museum the member already has any visit to (dated or not) is skipped (A§8).
- **Import** (P2c): generic CSV (a name, QID or museum.nl URL, a date, optionally a note; headers in NL and EN, `,` or `;`, dates `YYYY-MM-DD` or `D-M-YYYY`), MuseoTrack and Cultra exports [U formats: they wait for A10's samples], and `mjk` (our own JSON export, format v1: visits with notes, and the wishlist); at most 5,000 rows [U] and 2 MiB, else 413.
  - Rows are matched over visible museums: our museum id, then A§7.3 rule 1 (QID, `museumnl_url`), then a name rule (owner, Q3): exactly one normalised name or alias whose place, if the row has one, agrees; several are ambiguous. The matcher sorts rows into matched, ambiguous (the user picks) and unmatched (the user searches or skips; the candidates come from the screen's search, `GET /museums?q=…&limit=5`).
  - On confirmation they become `source = import` visits (and, for `mjk`, wishlist entries); an existing visit (same museum and date, undated included) is skipped.
  - From P3a the generic CSV also takes optional `rating` (`beoordeling`, `sterren`, `stars`; 1–5) and `review` (`recensie`) columns, the member's own words only: each creates a review on the imported visit through `POST /reviews`'s own create path (`createReview`: one per museum, an open museum, the member's visit). The preview already names `bad_rating`, `review_exists` (a second rated row for a museum, or a museum the member already reviewed) and `museum_closed`, and the review text rule's `too_long` (over 5,000 code points) and `bad_text`. At confirm each rated row runs in its own savepoint, so a refusal or a lost race applies nothing of that row, its new visit included. Each created review logs the client address (`content_ip_log`) and none counts against `review_user_day` (owner, Q7).
  - The data export (`export.json`) holds the member's reviews in any state, each with its revisions oldest first (owner, P3a Q10); the format stays version 1 and the importer ignores the key.
- **Export:** JSON of every row that holds the member's id or email, covering every category in A§9 "What is stored": account fields (email, handle, display name, created_at, the 16+ time, terms version and acceptance, settings), sessions (method, created, last used; no ids), passkey names and dates, visits with notes, wishlist, imports, reviews with their revisions, ratings, media metadata, `content_ip_log` rows, statements of reasons sent to them, and notices and appeals filed with their email; CSV of visits and wishlist; a ZIP of their photo originals (A§13.2). ## 11. Ratings and reviews

- **Rules** (D13, D17): one review per member per museum, citing one of the member's visits there, shown as "Bezocht op <date>" ("Bezocht" for an undated visit). Deleting the cited visit asks the member to pick another visit or delete the review. It needs a verified email and an active account. A closed museum's reviews are read-only.
- **Content:** overall 1–5 stars (required; drives the average). Optional aspects 1–5, stored as columns: `collection`, `presentation`, `kid_friendly`, `accessibility`, `facilities`, `value_for_money` (D13; the final list, owner, P3a Q1). Optional text: plain, any language, ≤ 5,000 code points, line breaks only (no HTML or Markdown), always rendered as one text node in `<p class="review-body" dir="auto">` (`white-space: pre-line`). One rule in `@mjk/contracts` and the same class in the CHECK (P3a): CRLF, CR, U+2028 and U+2029 become a line break and a TAB a space, the text is trimmed (empty is no text); any other control character, a lone surrogate and the bidi embeddings and isolates (U+202A–U+202E, U+2066–U+2069) are a 422 `["body"]`, never stripped silently. Length counts code points on both sides (`[...s].length`, `char_length`). Up to 10 photos (A§12).
- **Scores:** the museum page shows the plain mean and the count. Lists are ordered by the **Bayesian average** `(C·m + Σ ratings) / (C + n)` with C = 5 (owner, P3a Q3; `BAYES_C`) and m = the mean of the counted ratings (visible reviews of visible museums whose author is not deleted) [U tune after the beta] [U beta: with C = 5 a single 5-star review still outranks a 4.6 average once m > 4.5]. A museum without ratings scores m; a museum the recompute has not seen yet (a new one) has no `museum_stats` row and ranks last until it runs. Aspects show a mean only from 3 ratings up (owner, Q2; `ASPECT_MIN`). Every mean is rounded once, to one decimal, when it is stored; `min_rating=x` compares that shown mean (at least one rating), `kid_friendly=true` a kid-friendly mean ≥ 4 from at least 3 ratings (owner, Q6) [U P4]. The worker's `stats_recompute` job, coalesced (at most one queued job), with no delay, recomputes every museum in one statement after every review write, every sync or match that creates a museum and every hide or unhide: writers queue it under a shared advisory lock and at most one is queued (`job_stats_queued_idx`); the job takes that lock exclusive before it reads, so no committed change is left out, and a failed run is not retried (`max_attempts` 1; the next change queues a fresh one).
- **"Hide ratings until I've visited"** (the spoiler setting): on museums the member has not visited, scores and review texts stay folded behind "Toon beoordelingen". Public HTML is anonymous, so a tiny blocking same-origin script (not inline, `src/prefs.ts`) reads the `localStorage` key `mjk-hide-ratings` before the first paint and sets `<html data-hide-ratings>`, which CSS uses to fold every score, every review text and the review counts (`RatingFold`): no flash, no cookie, still cacheable. The flag is written at sign-in, re-synced from every `/api/v1/me` and `/api/v1/me/marks` answer and cleared on sign-out (P3a). Residuals, accepted (owner, P3a Q4): visited museums unfold once `/me/marks` answers (the marks know the visits, not `/me`); after a storage wipe (Safari clears script-written storage after 7 days without interaction) public pages show scores until the member opens a page that asks `/me` (`/paspoort`, `/account`); the list order still follows the scores. The setting's help text says so.
- **Edits:** allowed at any time. The previous version goes to `review_revision` (for notices and moderation, never public), and the review shows "bewerkt op <date>". The visit date a review shows is part of its content: citing a visit with another date, or changing the date of the cited visit, sets `edited_at` too (no revision: it keeps rating, aspects and body only).
- **Forbidden by the terms:** reviews by a museum's staff or for a reward, and more than one account per person.
- **"Hoe wij reviews controleren"** (linked beside every score, NL and EN): visits are self-declared, not checked; one review per member per museum; how the mean and the ranking are calculated; negative reviews are never suppressed or demoted, and moderation removes only illegal content and breaches of the terms; staff and rewarded reviews are forbidden; how to report a review.

## 12. Media (photos now, video P7)

- **Upload** (ADR-0008; the API answers in A§14):
  1. The browser calls `POST /api/v1/uploads` (declares kind, size, type, the museum and optionally the member's own review of it, and the three consents) → an id, the chunk size and the chunk count. As built in P3b: every quota runs in one transaction under one advisory lock, before any byte is stored (`apps/server/src/media/uploads.ts`): the type (415), 25 MiB and 1 GiB per member (422 `["size"]`), 10 per museum and member (= per review, a member has one review per museum; 422 `["photos"]`), 3 open (429 with `Retry-After`), 2 GiB declared across open uploads (503), then the limiter's `photo_user_day` (30, hit last, so a refused create counts nothing) and a `content_ip_log` row. The disk guard comes first (503 `unavailable`, A§17.7). Binary units throughout, because the chunk is 8 MiB.
  2. It sends `PUT /api/v1/uploads/<id>/chunks/<n>` in 8 MiB chunks, each far under Cloudflare's 100 MB body cap and its timeouts (documented on 2026-11-22: a 125 s read timeout for the origin's answer and a 30 s write timeout; whether the cap holds through the tunnel is not stated in so many words [U box P3b]). The `api` streams them to `/srv/mjk/quarantine/<id>/upload`; Caddy caps the body size per route.
     - Each `PUT` needs `0 ≤ n < chunk_count` and exactly the expected `Content-Length` (8 MiB, the last chunk the remainder), checked before any byte is written. It is written at offset n × 8 MiB into a file never larger than the declared size; anything else → 422 and nothing stored.
     - At most 3 uploads in `uploading` per member (else 429), and at most 2 GiB [U] declared across all open uploads (else 503).
     - As built in P3b: only this route takes `application/octet-stream` (any other type 415; `jsonOnly` names it); the body cap is 8 MiB in Caddy's `@chunk` and in the `api`; the body is streamed from `c.req.raw.body` (proven over a real socket with @hono/node-server, `apps/server/test/media/chunk-socket.int.test.ts`) with a byte counter and a running sha256 into positional writes inside the chunk's own range. A re-sent chunk is unreceived while it is written: the same bytes are accepted (204), other bytes refused (422 `["chunk"]`) and the chunk stays unreceived; a body that ends short or breaks off leaves it unreceived. One in-process lock per upload covers the chunk `PUT`, `complete` and `DELETE` (409 while busy; the `api` is one process).
  3. `POST /api/v1/uploads/<id>/complete`: size check, sha256 of the whole file, magic bytes with `file-type` 22.3.0 (JPEG, PNG, WebP only, and the declared type: an APNG is refused, an animated WebP keeps its first frame), the sha256 against `blocked_hash` (one `audit_log` row `media.blocked` that targets the member, the media id in its detail, so it outlives the failed photo's purge; the member's status shows only the neutral `upload_refused`), then a `media_process` job. Every refusal but a missing chunk fails the upload and removes its file, so nothing reaches the decoder.
  4. Unfinished uploads are purged after 24 h; a photo not attached to a review within 7 days is removed (owner, P3b N1).
- **Decoding** (owner, P3b Q1: the `media` role): sharp runs only in a `media` container of the server image with `network_mode: none`, no secret, no database login and one mount, `/srv/mjk/media-work` (a 256 MB tmpfs volume, A§17.1). The worker's `media_process` job copies the upload into a fresh task directory there and waits; the `media` role takes one task at a time and writes fixed-name outputs; the worker reads each output back through the task directory's descriptor (`/proc/self/fd/<fd>/<name>`, `O_NOFOLLOW`, `fstat`, one read into a buffer), validates the buffer (a strict `result.json`; each variant's container structure: AVIF or WebP with no metadata box or chunk and the expected dimensions, A§16.2) and writes it into quarantine itself, so a compromised decoder can neither reach a secret, the database or the network nor make the worker follow a link (A§16.2). sharp 0.35.5: `limitInputPixels` 50 MP, `sharp.concurrency(1)`, `sharp.cache(false)`, every libvips loader blocked but JPEG, PNG and WebP, `failOn: 'warning'`, one page, sharp's format equal to file-type's, 120 s [U] per photo from the moment the role takes it (a task not taken within 30 s is retried later, the upload kept; the role's watchdog kills the task's child at 130 s and the role stays up; a child that dies is `media_decode_failed` at once): orientation applied, then all metadata (EXIF, GPS, XMP, IPTC, ICC, comments, PNG text chunks) stripped, sRGB; AVIF and WebP at 400, 1200 and 2000 px wide plus the source width when it is smaller (never upscaled); a random 128-bit key per photo (`p/<key>/<width>.<ext>`), not derived from any id. The quarantine file is deleted when a job ends `failed`.
  - As built in P3b (`apps/server/src/media/{process,role,handoff,process-job}.ts`): libvips 8.18.7's loaders are blocked as a class (`VipsForeignLoad`) and exactly `VipsForeignLoadJpegFile`, `VipsForeignLoadPngFile` and `VipsForeignLoadWebpFile` unblocked (proven: an SVG, a TIFF, a GIF and an AVIF are refused although the build can read them). The header is read first: over 50 MP is `media_too_many_pixels` before any decode (a 30,000 × 30,000 PNG of a few hundred bytes), and sharp's format must equal the declared one. The file is decoded **once**, oriented and resized to the largest kept width, into raw sRGB pixels; every AVIF and WebP is encoded from those raw pixels, so no metadata has a path to an output (canaries in EXIF, GPS, XMP, IPTC, COM, ICC and PNG `tEXt`/`iTXt`/`eXIf` proven absent). Measured locally: a 12 MP JPEG 1.6 s, 9.3 s with heavy noise [U box: the VM's times and memory, A§17.2]. One deadline covers the seven pipelines (sharp's `timeout` gets what is left).
  - The task protocol: the worker writes `in`, `in.json` and `ready`; the role claims a task by renaming `ready` to `taken` and writes `<width>.<avif|webp>` and `result.json` last, each through a temporary name and a rename. A task not taken in 30 s is withdrawn by renaming `ready` (so a late claim and the give-up never both win) and retried (`media_not_taken`); 120 s after the take is `media_timeout`, not retried. On SIGTERM the role answers `media_aborted`, which the worker retries, so a deploy never fails a photo. The worker opens result files `O_NONBLOCK` too (a planted FIFO cannot block it) and removes the task through its descriptor, never by a recursive path walk. A re-claimed job also accepts a photo left in `processing`.
  - A photo is published only once it is attached to a review (P3b N1): processing a trusted member's attached photo, or attaching a trusted member's processed one, queues `media_publish`.

A successful `media_publish` deletes the quarantine copies and moves the original to `/srv/mjk/originals`; a rejection deletes the quarantine copies and the original. Nothing reaches R2 (the media bucket or the backup) before approval (A§13.1).

### 13.1 DSA

ACM, which supervises, says in its guidance for websites and apps (Handreiking DSA "Websites en apps", November 2024, §2.2; read 2026-11-22) that the DSA does not apply to a service with no direct or indirect profit motive at all: "Alleen als u geen enkel direct of indirect winstoogmerk heeft, geldt de DSA niet." That is this site while D14 holds; a free service that earns in any way is in scope. It is regulator guidance, not case law, so the item stays on the A9 list. The plan builds the hosting duties below anyway, as good practice: a site outside the DSA also has no claim on its liability exemption [U A9], and acting fast on a notice is then the whole position.

- **No postal address:** art. 3:15d BW (name and address of establishment) has the same threshold, "a service normally provided for remuneration". So `/contact` and `/privacy` give the owner's name and an email address, and no home address. The two go together: a text that claims the site is in the DSA's scope would bring the address duty with it, so the legal texts describe the notice, statement and appeal flows as what the site does, never as duties under the DSA.
- **Any revenue changes this** (D14): ads, affiliate links, sponsorship or paid features put the site in scope of both.

### 13.3 Copyright and portrait rights

- **No panorama freedom inside a museum:** Aw art. 18 covers only works placed permanently in public places, which museums are not [U]. Art. 18a allows incidental inclusion. Copyright runs 70 years after the author's death (art. 37). Portraits: Aw art. 21.
- **Terms (upload licence):** the user keeps the copyright and warrants they took the photo and hold the rights. They grant a non-exclusive, royalty-free licence to store, transcode and display it on the site, credited by handle. The licence ends on deletion (backups age out, A§13.2).
- **At upload:** the three checkboxes (A§12) and the museum's `photo_policy` (house rules are contractual). A `forbidden` policy is a prominent warning, not a refusal (owner, P3b Q4): the member decides, and the queue and notices handle what goes wrong.
- **Takedown:** the Art. 16 form offers the `notice.category` enum (A§8): copyright, portrait right, privacy, defamation, CSAM, threat, other illegal content, terms breach. Repeat infringers are banned [U threshold].
- **Approval and liability** [U A9] (added 2026-11-22): whether approving a queued photo by hand counts as knowing of an infringement in a photographed artwork is on the A9 list; Pictoright licenses such uses for visual artists. Until it is settled, a moderator rejects a photo that mainly shows one artwork whose maker is alive or died less than 70 years ago, which is what the third upload checkbox already asks of the member.
- **Our own sources:** no museum.nl text, images or list extraction (database right, A§7.1). "Museumkaart" only descriptively, plus the "not affiliated" footer (ADR-0006).

### 13.4 Reviews and consumer law

- **Not a trader** (D14, ADR-0016) [U A9], so the UCPD review duties do not bind; they are followed as good practice: "Hoe wij reviews controleren" beside every score, the staff and reward ban, no suppression of negative reviews (A§11).
- **Defamation:** the reviewer is primarily liable. A court can order the reviewer's identity disclosed (Lycos/Pessers, HR 2005), which is why the email and the 30-day IP log are kept.
- **Disclaimer:** museum information is given without guarantee; "report a change" corrects it.

### 13.5 Account deletion choice

Settings → delete account → a fresh email code → the choice (D10):

| | Delete everything | Keep reviews as "voormalig lid" |
|---|---|---|
| Profile, sessions, passkeys, visits, wishlist, settings, exports | Deleted | Deleted |
| Photos (variants on R2, pending copies in quarantine, originals) | Deleted | Deleted |
| Reviews and ratings | Deleted | Kept without photos; `user_id` cleared, shown as "voormalig lid"; revisions deleted; no longer editable |
| P6 comments, votes, follows | Deleted | Deleted |

- The page warns that kept review text can still identify the author [U].
- A deletion job runs in the worker: the user turns `deleted` and loses every session at once, and the rows go within minutes. R2 variants, quarantine copies and originals are deleted at once; edge copies expire within 4 h (A§12). Then `museum_stats` is recomputed and a last email confirms it (its address sealed in the job, A§9).
- **Not erased** (Art. 17(3)(e), legal claims): notices and moderation actions about the user, with the user id set to null, for 3 years [U]; notices the member filed, for 3 years [U]; `content_ip_log` rows until their 30 days end; `audit_log` rows, which hold ids only.
- Backups age out within 35 days [U]. Deletions since the snapshot are re-applied from production's `audit_log` when it survives. For a full loss, they come from the owner's deletion notices: the deletion job mails the owner "<account id> deleted <time>" (ids only) [U]. The restore runbook keeps the site in maintenance until those deletions are re-run (A§17.6).
- The handle stays reserved for 6 months [U]. Nothing can be undone.

- **Shapes:** the A§8 column names (snake_case, no mapping layer, as F1), times as ISO-8601 UTC strings, visit dates `YYYY-MM-DD`, money in integer cents. Every answer has a strict zod contract in `@mjk/contracts`. An integration test parses each route against it, the `ssr` loaders return the same contracts (A§15.1), and the web parses every answer (jitless Zod).
- **Requests:** query strings and bodies parse with strict schemas, so an unknown parameter or key → 400. Mutations take only `application/json` (else 415); the upload chunks only `application/octet-stream`. Body caps at Caddy and in the `api`: 64 KB, imports 2 MB, chunks 8 MiB. The `api`'s cap reads only the headers (P1b): a `Content-Length` over the cap → 413 `too_large`, and a `Transfer-Encoding` (a body of unknown length) → 400 (kept in P2a). `/api/admin/*` answers 404 before the cap. As built in P2a: after the cap, every unsafe method meets the CSRF check (403), then the JSON-only check (415: a `Content-Type` other than `application/json`, or a non-zero body without one); a body-less POST (sign-out) needs neither a length nor a type; the one body reader (`apps/server/src/api/body.ts`) compares the bytes it reads with the `Content-Length` and parses with a strict schema. The request log line carries a fixed `body` field (`none` · `length` · `chunked` · `mismatch`), never the content, so the tunnel check below can be read from the logs.
  - **Lengths through the tunnel** (cloudflared's source and its issues #544, #1485 and #1511, read 2026-11-22): a body the browser sent with a `Content-Length` reaches the origin with that length. A body sent without one arrives chunked, or on some edge and protocol combinations empty with `Content-Length: 0`, since Cloudflare's edge stopped buffering uploads in 2025; and a body-less request can lose its `Content-Length: 0`. So no route needs a length on a body-less request, a length-less body stays a 400, every route that reads a body also counts the bytes it reads, and P2a's [box] criterion proves the lengths on the real tunnel before P3b's chunk check (A§12) relies on them [U box, after P2a's merge: `docs/runbooks/accounts.md` B4; the result goes here].
- **CSRF** (A§9): `SameSite=Lax`, plus our own middleware (`apps/server/src/api/middleware/csrf.ts`, P2a) on every unsafe method (all but GET, HEAD and OPTIONS) that requires `Sec-Fetch-Site: same-origin`, or, when the browser sent no `Sec-Fetch-Site`, an `Origin` equal to this host's own origin (from MJK_DOMAIN and the host class, never the request's `Host`); `same-site` is refused, since the two hosts are same-site; else 403. hono's `csrf()` is not used (it covers form types only [U, not needed]). No CORS headers.
- **Status codes:** 200 · 201 · 202 (a mail or a job follows) · 204 · 301 (an old slug or handle) · 400 `bad_request` · 401 `unauthorized` · 403 `forbidden` / `terms_required` / `reauth_required` / `banned` · 404 `not_found` · 409 `conflict` · 413 `too_large` · 415 `unsupported_type` · 422 `invalid` · 429 `rate_limited` with `Retry-After` · 500 `internal` · 503 `unavailable`.
- **Errors are fixed:** `{"error":"<code>"}`, and on 400 and 422 a `fields` list with the names of the failing schema paths, never their values. No answer or log echoes input, a header, a token, a code, an email or a database message. Auth answers never tell whether an email has an account, and take the same time (A§9).
- **Paging:** keyset everywhere, `?cursor=<opaque>&limit=1..50` (default 20) → `{"items":[…],"next_cursor":…}`. The cursor is base64url of the last sort key plus the id (with `q`: the tier and score too), at most 4,096 characters, decoded through a strict schema before any SQL; a malformed one, or one of the other mode, → 400. No offsets.
- **Cache-Control** (set by the `api` per answer):
  - public GETs: `public, max-age=0, s-maxage=60` [U P5a tunes it; Cloudflare caches JSON only through the Cache Rules of A§17.7]; `/museums/points` `s-maxage=300`; a 301 `s-maxage=3600`
  - `/me*`, `/auth/*`, `/uploads*`, `/museums/near`, every mutation, every 4xx and 5xx, and all of `/api/admin/*`: `private, no-store`
  - a removed review can stay at the edge for up to the `s-maxage`, a removed photo's variants for up to the 4 h edge TTL (A§12); an urgent removal purges by URL
- **Rate limits:** the A§9 table; a refusal is a 429 and logs only the bucket's name.
- **Request logs:** one line each: method, route pattern (`/museums/:slug`, never the raw path or query), status, duration, request id (invariant 8). The `api` generates the request id itself and returns it as `X-Request-Id`; a client's header is ignored. The pattern is the first matched route that is not a global middleware (Hono 4.13 lists `app.use('*')` as `/*`), or `unmatched` (P1b).

## 15. Frontend

### 15.1 Server rendering and hydration

- One Vite 8 build of `apps/web` (`vite build`, or `buildWeb()` in `apps/web/build.ts`), three outputs (ADR-0009):
  - the client bundle (`src/entry-client.tsx`) in `dist/client`. There is no `index.html`: the SSR bundle writes the whole document and finds the hashed files through Vite's manifest;
  - the SSR bundle (`src/entry-server.tsx`) as one self-contained `dist/server/entry-server.js` with production React, which the `ssr` role imports;
  - the admin entry (`admin.html`, `src/admin/main.tsx`) from P2a.
- **Server:** `ssr` (Hono `:8081`) matches the path with the shared `routeOf`, runs the route's loader on the `pub_*` views as `mjk_ssr`, and renders with React 19's `prerender` from `react-dom/static` (P1c, owner): the data is loaded first, so nothing suspends, the status and headers are known before the first byte, and the output is one finished document. Each render runs in its own `AsyncLocalStorage` locale, which Paraglide 2.29's `getLocale()` reads through `overwriteServerAsyncLocalStorage`, so concurrent NL and EN renders never mix. The head holds title, description, canonical, `hreflang`, Open Graph and JSON-LD `Museum`; the content is in the body. The SSR bundle gets only the path and query, the api's answers and the config, never a request.
- **Same data, same contract:** `ssr` runs the api's own catalogue routes in-process on its `mjk_ssr` pool (`mountCatalogue`, `inProcessApi`, P1c), so a loader returns exactly what the matching `/api/v1` GET returns: the same parse, query function, contract check and reshaping. A test per page kind compares the two. The TanStack Query key is the `/api/v1` path. `<script type="application/json" id="mjk-state">` holds `{"<api path>": <the api's JSON>}` in the page's fetch order: no TanStack timestamps, so the HTML stays byte-identical. The client seeds its cache from it, calls `hydrateRoot` and fetches nothing it already has (`staleTime: Infinity`, no refetch on mount, focus or reconnect).
- **Data blocks, no inline script:** `mjk-state` and the JSON-LD (`application/ld+json`) are the only `<script>` elements without `src`. Neither executes, so `script-src 'self'` holds. Both are serialised with `<`, `>`, `&`, U+2028 and U+2029 escaped as `\u` sequences (invariant 5). The SSR test allows exactly those two types.
- **Anonymous by construction:** Caddy strips `Cookie` and `Authorization` before proxying to `ssr`, and `ssr` sets no cookie. Public HTML is byte-identical with or without a session (P1c test) and cacheable: `Cache-Control: public, max-age=0, s-maxage=60` [U P5a tunes it; Cloudflare caches HTML only through the Cache Rules of A§17.7].
- **Personal bits after hydration:** the account menu, visited badges, the wishlist button and the member's own review come from `/api/v1/me` and `/api/v1/me/museums/:slug`, in slots that keep their size (no layout shift). P2a's header has one neutral "Account" link instead of an account menu, the same for everyone, so a public page makes no `/me` call; `/account` sends a signed-out visitor to `/inloggen`.
- **The member's marks on public pages** (P2c): after hydration, from `GET /me/marks`, and only while a local "signed in" hint (one `localStorage` key, set whenever `GET /me` answers a member and cleared when it or `GET /me/marks` answers signed out) is set, so an anonymous visitor makes no API call. The marks are never in `mjk-state`, and the filters "visited" and "wishlist" are client state, never in the URL.
- **Logged-in routes** (`/paspoort`, `/verlanglijst`, `/welkom`, `/account`, `/inloggen`) get the app shell: one HTML for everyone, `noindex`; the SPA takes over.
- **The prefs script** (A§11, built in P3a; owner, P1c Q6): `/assets/prefs-<hash>.js` (`src/prefs.ts`, a third client input with no import or export, so it runs as a classic script), tiny, blocking, same-origin, emitted right after the two `<meta>` elements of every hydrated document (error documents stay script-free). It reads the `localStorage` flag of the ratings fold and sets `data-hide-ratings` on `<html>` before the first paint. The theme flag joins it in P5a (owner, P3a N1); until then the theme follows `prefers-color-scheme` only.
- **Hydration rules:** dates through `Intl.DateTimeFormat` in Europe/Amsterdam on both sides (F1); no `style=` in server markup (CLAUDE.md gotcha); no clock or random values during render. A hydration warning fails Playwright (P1c).
- **Errors:** an unknown route or museum → the SSR 404 page with status 404; an invalid filter → 400; an old slug → 301 to the current page (`s-maxage=3600`); a render failure → the 500 page without details, logged as `render_failed`; the database down → 503. Error documents are static: no client script, no `mjk-state`, `private, no-store`, `noindex`. In the SPA an error boundary shows "Er ging iets mis: herlaad de pagina."

### 15.3 Map

- maplibre-gl 6.15.0 and pmtiles 4.5.0 in a lazy chunk, loaded only on `/kaart` and `/en/map` (`lib/map/createMap.ts` is the one module that imports them; `apps/web/test/build.test.ts` checks the manifest: about 1.05 MB of JS, 70 kB of CSS and a 506 kB worker, none of it in the entry's static imports); a museum page links to the map instead of embedding one. The worker URL comes from Vite's `?worker&url` through `setWorkerUrl()`: same origin, `worker-src 'self'`, no `blob:` (Waterheight ADR-0016). The map component returns on `import.meta.env.SSR`, so the SSR build emits no map chunk (P1d).
- **Tiles:** a Protomaps extract of the Netherlands, the `NL_BOX` of `packages/contracts` (lat 50.7–53.7, lon 3.2–7.3) at the build's full zoom range (owner, P1d Q1; its size, ~1–2 GB, is [U box P1d]), made by the quarterly `tiles_refresh` worker job (the first Sunday of January, April, July and October at 02:23 Europe/Amsterdam, owner Q5; by hand `admin tiles refresh`) with `go-pmtiles extract` into `/srv/mjk/tiles-build` (A§17.1, emptied after the upload and after a failure), checked (PMTiles v3, MVT, bounds, size) and uploaded with the own SigV4 client (`apps/server/src/media/r2.ts`, `r2_media_rw`) as `media.<domain>/tiles/nl-<build>.pmtiles` (`application/vnd.pmtiles`, immutable): multipart in 32 MiB parts, aborted on any failure, while a small object is one PUT of at most 64 MiB (owner Q4). Then `tiles/current.json` = `{"file": …, "previous": … | null}` (`max-age=300`) flips to the new file; the previous file stays for rollback until the next refresh succeeds, then it is deleted (owner Q3); `admin tiles rollback` swaps `file` and `previous`. The browser reads it with range requests (`connect-src https://media.<domain>`); R2's CORS rule allows `GET` and `HEAD` from `https://<domain>` only, with `AllowedHeaders: range` for browsers that preflight a `Range` and `ExposeHeaders: ETag` for pmtiles (the e2e stand-in applies exactly this rule; the bucket's own behaviour is [U box P1d]); pmtiles 4.5.0 sends no `If-Match`, so the rule needs no `if-match`.
- **Tiles and Cloudflare's cache** (Cloudflare and Protomaps docs, 2026-11-22; [U box P1d] until measured): Free, Pro and Business cache objects up to 512 MB, judged by the complete size from `Content-Range`, so the 1–2 GB file is never edge-cached and a warm range read is no `HIT`. `.pmtiles` is not a default cached extension: without a rule Cloudflare passes the browser's `Range` on and R2 answers 206 (`cf-cache-status` `DYNAMIC` or `MISS`, cold and warm alike). So every tile request is an R2 GetObject, a Class B operation (10 million a month free, then $0.36 per million), and pays R2's latency (Protomaps warns of 500 ms or more). **Never a cache-eligible rule that covers `tiles/` while the file is over 512 MB** (owner Q7 answered by the limit): on a cacheable miss Cloudflare may drop the `Range` and pull the complete file from R2, which it then does not store. The [box] run records the size, the time to first byte of ten cold range reads and the Class B reads of one phone session (`docs/runbooks/tiles-refresh.md` §8). If the map is slow on a phone over 4G, the owner chooses: accept it; cap the extract under 512 MB (drop the top zoom, MapLibre overzooms) and test whether a Cache Rule on `tiles/*` with "Origin Range Requests" then gives `HIT`s (untested: that setting fetches 1 MiB-aligned ranges but makes nothing cacheable, and the limit applies to the whole file); or a Worker in front that serves edge-cached tiles, as Protomaps recommends (a new component, so an ADR).
- **Style:** generated from `@protomaps/basemaps` 5.7.2 (flavour `light`) by `scripts/build-map-style.ts`, validated against the style spec 26.4.4, committed, one per language (`name:nl` / `name:en`). Glyphs (Noto Sans Regular, Medium and Italic, all 256 ranges, OFL-1.1) and the `light` sprites (MIT) are byte-for-byte copies of `protomaps/basemaps-assets` at 028c18f with a `SHA256SUMS`, served by Caddy under `/map/028c18f/*` (immutable; the admin host serves none). basemaps' Devanagari font stack becomes Noto Sans Regular, so no label asks for a font we do not serve. Attribution "© OpenStreetMap contributors · Protomaps", plain text with no link (owner, P1d); the pmtiles protocol runs with `metadata: false`, because MapLibre's attribution control renders HTML. The basemap stays light in dark mode.
- **Museum points:** `GET /api/v1/museums/points` (the list's filters) as a GeoJSON source with MapLibre's clustering. Museumkaart museums are a diamond, the others a circle, in colours from `styles.css` that `contrast.test.ts` holds at ≥ 3:1 against every fill of the style (never colour alone); members also see visited markers (squares) and wishlist markers (triangles), each in its own colour held at the same contrast (P2c). A Museumkaart museum the member has visited or wishlisted shows the member's marker (square or triangle) instead of the diamond; its card still names the Museumkaart status (owner, 2026-11-27: the member's own marker wins over the Museumkaart cue). Tap a point → a card rendered by React (the name in the page language, the Museumkaart badge, a link) → the page; never a MapLibre popup. The list filters apply to the map: the map page keeps the list's query.
- **Near me:** only on a button press, through the browser's Geolocation API. The position is rounded to 3 decimals (about 100 m; owner, P1d Q2, which P1b's `NearQuery` enforces) before `GET /api/v1/museums/near` and is never stored, put in the page URL or logged (`deploy/e2e/stack.sh` checks the container logs). Denied, unavailable, outside the Netherlands or an API failure → a message and the list. `Permissions-Policy` allows geolocation for our origin only (A§15.6).
- **Not the only way:** the map page is the filter form and the paged list of the same museums, server-rendered, with the map added after hydration; without JavaScript, without WebGL2 and from the keyboard the list is the way (WCAG 2.5.7). `prefers-reduced-motion` turns off fly-to animations and fades.

### 15.6 CSP and security headers

Public host `<domain>`, set by Caddy on every response (error responses included: routes use their own `respond`, and `handle_errors` repeats the headers on Caddy's error path, A§17.2; CLAUDE.md gotcha):

```
Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none'; script-src 'self'; style-src 'self'; img-src 'self' data: https://media.<domain>; media-src https://media.<domain>; connect-src 'self' https://media.<domain>; worker-src 'self'; font-src 'self'; manifest-src 'self'; form-action 'self'; frame-ancestors 'none'; base-uri 'none'
```

- `img-src data:` serves only the SVG icons in MapLibre's CSS: without it every Waterheight map spec failed (Waterheight ADR-0016; our ADR-0009). The build test still fails on any other `data:` or `blob:` URL.
- `connect-src https://media.<domain>` is for the PMTiles range requests; `media-src` for video from P7 (harmless before).
- Never `'unsafe-inline'`, `'unsafe-eval'`, `'wasm-unsafe-eval'`, `blob:` or a third-party host: Zod runs `jitless`, React sets styles only through the CSSOM, ALTCHA uses its `/dist/external` build, MapLibre a same-origin worker. A change needs an ADR (invariant 4).
- **One stricter exception** (P3b): `GET /api/v1/uploads/<id>/preview`, a pending photo's WebP read from quarantine, carries the media host's `default-src 'none'; sandbox` instead of the page CSP. Caddy sets it from two sibling deferred `header` blocks on the exclusive matchers `@preview` and `@not_preview` (defined once in the `:80` site): a deferred block nested deeper is overwritten by the outer one, which is applied last (CLAUDE.md gotcha). `deploy/e2e/caddy-cases.mjs` proves it through the real Caddy. Photos need nothing new in the page CSP: published variants come from `https://media.<domain>` (`img-src`), the preview from `'self'`; the uploader shows no `blob:` preview.
- Other headers:
  - `Strict-Transport-Security: max-age=31536000; includeSubDomains` (preload [U] once the brand is final; Waterheight ADR-0020)
  - `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff` · `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin`
  - `Permissions-Policy: geolocation=(self), camera=(), microphone=(), payment=(), usb=(), browsing-topics=()`
  - `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin` · `Cross-Origin-Resource-Policy: same-origin`
  - `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` on every response until P5b (prelaunch profile); then only on `/api/*`, the app shell, private routes, search results (`noindex, follow`) and `/melden` (launch profile; the admin host keeps `noindex, nofollow`)
  - `Cache-Control` per A§14 and A§15.1; no `Set-Cookie` but `__Host-mjk_sid` from the auth routes
- **WebKit and `<select>`** (P1c): Playwright 1.64.1's WebKit raises one `style-src-attr` violation of its own for every `<select>`, plus a console error, even on a static page with no script. Chromium raises none. This is no style of ours, and the policy stays as it is. The e2e guard skips that first report per `<select>` element, in WebKit only, and that many of the console errors (A§18). Whether Safari does the same is [U the owner's phone check of the list filters, P1c]. A `report-to` endpoint (below) would then count one `style-src-attr` per `<select>` from Safari.
- **ALTCHA under the CSP** (proven in P2a): the widget 3.5.0's `/dist/external` build and its SHA-256 worker (`altcha/workers/sha`, a same-origin Vite worker) hold no WebAssembly and no `eval` (checked in the tarball), and its stylesheet is a file, not an injected `<style>`. - **Passkeys under the CSP** (P2b): @simplewebauthn/browser 14.0.0 has no dependencies and is bundled into the one lazy chunk `src/lib/passkey.ts`, reached only through `import()` from the sign-in and account screens (never from either entry, never in `dist/server`); `build.test.ts` fails on `eval(`, `new Function`, `WebAssembly` or `blob:` in it, and its one absolute URL is a documentation link inside a console text (allowed by name). The ceremonies need no CSP change: WebAuthn is not governed by `connect-src`, and the Permissions-Policy leaves `publickey-credentials-get` and `-create` at their default, `self`.
- A CSP `report-to` endpoint that counts violations per directive and stores nothing else [U P5a decides]; none until then.
- **Admin host** `admin.<domain>`: `default-src 'none'; script-src 'self'; style-src 'self'; img-src 'self' https://media.<domain>; connect-src 'self'; worker-src 'self'; font-src 'self'; form-action 'self'; frame-ancestors 'none'; base-uri 'none'` (`worker-src` for ALTCHA at sign-in; no map and no manifest; `media-src https://media.<domain>` joins in P7 for the video queue), plus `Cache-Control: private, no-store`, `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow`, `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` and the other headers above.
- **Media host** `media.<domain>` (R2, a Transform Rule, A2): `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff` and `Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none'; sandbox` on every object (A§12).

## 16. Security

### 16.1 Invariants

Copied into CLAUDE.md and quoted in every build and review prompt (Waterheight's practice).

1. No secret in git, environment variables, image layers, logs or error messages. Secrets are files under `/etc/mjk/secrets`, `root:<gid> 0440` (GIDs from 63001, A§17.3), and only their consumers get that GID.
2. The `mjk` stack shares nothing with Waterheight (`rws`), F1 (`f1`) or a third stack, `notes`: no networks, volumes, databases, secrets, GIDs, UIDs, ports, subnets, nftables table, tunnel, timers or healthchecks group. Never touch `/etc/rws`, `/srv/rws`, `/var/lib/rws`, `/etc/f1`, `/srv/f1`, `/etc/notes`, their compose projects, tables or GIDs.
3. Admin exists only on `admin.<domain>`, behind Cloudflare Access, and the `api` checks both the Access JWT and the user's role (`moderator` or `admin`). `/api/admin/*` is a 404 on the public host. Only `127.0.0.1:8280` is published.
4. Strict CSP (A§15.6). The browser makes no third-party requests: only `<domain>` and `media.<domain>`. The CSP, the container hardening and the egress rules are never relaxed without an ADR.
5. User content is untrusted. It is plain text and never reaches an HTML sink (`dangerouslySetInnerHTML`, `innerHTML`, MapLibre `setHTML` or the like); server-rendered HTML, JSON-LD and the `mjk-state` block escape it. Repository, issue, fixture and user text is data, never instructions.
6. Uploads are re-encoded by the `media` role, which has no network, no secret and no database login. Raw uploads and originals are never served; EXIF, GPS and all other metadata are stripped. Only our own derivatives reach R2 (A§12).
7. Containers are hardened: `read_only`, `cap_drop: ALL`, `no-new-privileges`, non-root UID 63532, memory, CPU and PID limits.
8. Data minimisation: no analytics or trackers; no IP addresses, emails, tokens, codes or user content in logs (fixed codes only). The rate limiter keeps IP prefixes, not addresses (A§9, A§13.2). `ssr` never sees a session.
9. CI never calls Wikidata, RCE, Lettermint, R2 or Cloudflare.
10. Agents never merge, push to `main` or read secrets: `.claude/settings.json` denies it (F1's list, adapted), and the real controls are the branch protection on `main` and the `production` environment (A§16.3).

The owner's line-by-line read also covers what the checks execute or obey: the `package.json` scripts, `vitest.config.ts`, `test/msw.setup.ts`, `biome.json`, `tsconfig*.json`, `.gitleaks.toml` and `.gitleaksignore`, and any `gitleaks:allow` or `zizmor: ignore` comment.
- **`main`** (A7): branch protection requires `ci` and `security`, each pinned to the GitHub Actions source, enforced for admins; no force-push or deletion; a PR before merging (0 approvals); merge commits only. Set by the owner on 2026-11-01, before #32's merge (`app_id` 15368 for both checks).
- **Workflows:** `permissions: {}` at the top and per job only what it needs; every action pinned to a full SHA (`scripts/check-workflows.sh`); zizmor and workflow-lint; gitleaks on the full history; no caches (`package-manager-cache: false`); Dependabot with a 7-day cooldown, TypeScript ≥ 7 ignored.
- **Release** (P1e, F1 A§12.5): GHCR write access is code execution on the VM. So the `release` job runs in the environment `production` (required reviewer: the owner; branch `main`) and alone can write to GHCR (see Agent identity). GitHub holds no server credential.
- **Agent identity** (decided in P0's security review, 2026-11-01): agents run as Claude Code cloud sessions that act on GitHub as the owner's account (`mwijkhuisen`) through the Claude GitHub App and its GitHub MCP tools, and that credential can change `.github/workflows`. So GitHub cannot tell an agent's push, PR or merge from the owner's. With 0 approvals, the branch protection on `main` does not stop an agent merge once `ci` and `security` are green: the deny list in `.claude/settings.json`, including the GitHub MCP merge and file-write tools, is the only barrier to an agent merge, and `ci` and `security` prove only what the PR's own files define. Accepted for P0–P1d, where nothing is deployed and the repository holds no secret. Before P1e's `release` job first pushes to GHCR, one of these holds, and P1e's [owner] criterion checks it: (1) the agent credential cannot change `.github/workflows`: the Workflows permission is removed, or, if the Claude GitHub App cannot drop it for this repository, agents push with a fine-grained token without the Administration and Workflows permissions, as F1 did; or (2) GHCR write never comes from a `GITHUB_TOKEN` that a branch's workflow can request: the packages give this repository's Actions read access only, and the `release` job pushes with a `write:packages` credential held only as a `production` environment secret. Condition (2) is the one that holds (owner, 2026-11-22, P1e review S4): the credential is the owner's own classic personal access token with the scope `write:packages` only (`repo` unticked), an expiry of at most 90 days and a rotation before it (`docs/runbooks/secrets-rotation.md` §7), stored only as the `production` secret `GHCR_PUSH_TOKEN`, which only the `release` job on `main` can read after the owner's approval.   - **SHA pinning enforced only by files a PR can change: confirmed.** At A7 the owner turned on the Actions policy "Require actions to be pinned to a full-length commit SHA" (`sha_pinning_required: true`) and allows only `actions/checkout` and `actions/setup-node` (`allowed_actions: selected`; owner, 2026-11-01). ### 17.4 Firewall

- A separate `table inet mjk`, loaded by `mjk-firewall.service` (oneshot, `nft -c` then `nft -f`, never `flush ruleset`). Forward hook on `mjk-*` interfaces only:
  - DNS from the four egress networks (`mjk-egress`, `mjk-api-egress`, `mjk-tunnel`, `mjk-backup-egress`) to `resolvers4`, before the drop of private ranges (the resolvers may be on the LAN)
  - tcp/443 from `mjk-egress` (worker) to anything but private ranges (to the sets below from P3b)
  - tcp/443 from `mjk-api-egress` (the Access certs) and `mjk-backup-egress` (R2) to `cf4` (R2's `.eu.` S3 endpoint is inside it: the first backup run on the VM passed, 2026-11-26)
  - tcp+udp/7844 from `mjk-tunnel` to `cf_tunnel4`
  - drop everything else, all of `mjk-publish`'s egress included, plus host-input drops for `mjk-*` (only established/related replies pass: docker-proxy's path for 8280)
- Private ranges are dropped even on 443. Traffic between two `mjk-*` bridges is `return`ed to Docker's own rules.
- On the VM (2026-11-26) the host-input drop `containers never open a connection to the host` rose by a few packets now and then at rest (390 to 398 over an evening, 5 in one minute), not on a container start; the containers have no IPv6 address. A minute of logging caught none, which says little at a few packets an evening, so the source is not identified yet [U]. It does not matter to the probes (each judges only the counter its own packets should move), but the drop is also where a real container-to-host attempt would show, so the source must be identified before invites go beyond the owner and test accounts [U].
- Sets: `cf4` from `https://www.cloudflare.com/ips-v4`; `cf_tunnel4` = 198.41.192.0/24 and 198.41.200.0/24 (Cloudflare's "Tunnel with firewall" page); `resolvers4`, filled by `mjk-resolvers` from the host's resolv.conf at start and on every `mjk-tick`. The owner refreshes `cf4` and `cf_tunnel4` by hand (`docs/runbooks/cloudflare.md`). P1e wrote both from the agent's knowledge, not fetched (agents never call Cloudflare) [U box: check `cf4` against the live list]. `cf_tunnel4` proven on the VM (2026-11-26): every address of `region1.v2.argotunnel.com` and `region2.v2.argotunnel.com` (20) lies inside it, and the tunnel connected through it. A table load empties `resolvers4`, so `mjk-firewall.service` and `mjk-tick` refill it; `mjk-resolvers` reads `/etc/resolv.conf`, or `/run/systemd/resolve/resolv.conf` when the first lists only loopback resolvers (the file Docker uses then). The unit has no `ExecStop`: stopping it leaves the table loaded (fail closed). It is `PartOf=nftables.service` and `ReloadPropagatedFrom=nftables.service`, `After=nftables.service`, and `nftables.service` wants it. From the units alone: a restart of nftables restarts the unit after nftables has started again, and a reload of nftables reloads it, so both should load the table again [U: neither is tested; `deploy/tests/e2e.sh` does not exercise them]. A stop of nftables stops the unit first (no `ExecStop`, nothing happens), then nftables empties the ruleset, and nothing loads `table inet mjk` until the next `mjk-tick`; a start of nftables while the unit is active (`RemainAfterExit`) does not run it again either. `mjk-tick` (`OnCalendar=*:07/10`, `AccuracySec=30s`) loads a missing table, so the gap is at most about 10 minutes, with the stack's egress open meanwhile. On the VM (2026-11-26) `nftables.service` is masked and inactive, and its `/etc/nftables.conf` begins by emptying the whole ruleset: a start or reload would delete every table on the host, Docker's, Waterheight's, F1's and the `notes` stack's included. So it stays masked on this shared host, and none of this applies there while it does (`docs/runbooks/install.md` §8). Container DNS (proven on the VM, 2026-11-26, per-service `nslookup` probes and a host-side capture, install.md §8): Docker's embedded DNS asks the host's resolvers from the host's namespace (the VM's own address on its uplink) even when they are not on loopback, so a container's lookups pass the table and `resolvers4`; an explicit `dns:` is asked from the container's namespace instead (a TEST-NET `dns:` left on the container's own address and was dropped here). `caddy` has `dns: 127.0.0.1` (A§16.2); `ssr`, internal-only, gets SERVFAIL; `api`, `worker`, `sync-museums`, `backup` and `cloudflared` resolve through the host. No file under `deploy/` may hold the words "flush" and "ruleset" next to each other (`nft-check.sh`).
- `table inet rws` and `table inet f1` match only their own bridges: without this table the stack's egress would be open.
- Health pings: the worker's from `worker` over `mjk-egress`; the backup's from the host unit `mjk-backup.service` (`mjk-ping result` from `ExecStopPost`: `/mjk-backup` on success, `/fail` otherwise), with the key on `curl -K -` stdin, never in argv (owner, P1e Q5). The backup container has no `hc_ping_key`.
- P3b narrows the worker's egress to sets (owner, P3b Q3; `deploy/host/nftables/mjk.nft`): the `mjk-egress` network's tcp/443 goes only to `@cf4` (R2 and any Cloudflare-fronted host) and to `@worker_dst4`, the IPv4 addresses of the hosts in `/etc/mjk/egress-hosts` (`deploy/host/egress-hosts`: `query.wikidata.org`, `services.rce.geovoorziening.nl`, `commons.wikimedia.org`, `upload.wikimedia.org`, `api.lettermint.co`, `hc-ping.com`, `build-metadata.protomaps.dev`, `build.protomaps.com`; `test/egress-hosts.test.ts` holds the list equal to the hosts the code dials). The set sources, resolved: `mjk-egress` looks each name up with `getent ahostsv4`, the host's resolver, which is also what Docker's embedded DNS answers the worker from, and leaves out an address in `private4`. When every host resolves it replaces the set (flush and add in one `nft -f`); when some fail it only adds, so a passing lookup failure never locks a host out; when all fail it changes nothing. No element timeouts. A table load empties the set, so the worker reaches only `cf4` until the next fill (fail closed). `mjk-egress` runs from its own oneshot `mjk-egress.service` (P3b security review: `Wants=` from `mjk-firewall.service`, `After=` it, `PartOf=` and `ReloadPropagatedFrom=` it, and never `Before=docker.service`, so its lookups, up to 8 × 5 s, never hold up Docker, whose daemon runs every stack on the VM), from `mjk-tick` step 2 every 10 minutes and from `mjk-install` when only the list changed (`mjk-install` also starts the unit, a no-op once it ran; under `MJK_ROOT` there is no systemctl and the tests run the script directly). For the lookups `mjk-egress.service` and `mjk-tick.service` allow `AF_INET` and `AF_INET6` in `RestrictAddressFamilies`, next to `AF_NETLINK AF_UNIX`, with `CAP_NET_ADMIN` only; `mjk-firewall.service`, which is `Before=docker.service`, keeps `AF_NETLINK AF_UNIX` and runs no lookup. A widening of two host units' sandbox, not of any container's [U box: the units' DNS on the VM, and the reload propagation, `docs/runbooks/photos.md`]. `e2e.sh` step 4 proves it with one literal address: tcp/443 outside both sets is dropped, to a `worker_dst4` address it passes, tcp/80 to it and the `api`'s tcp/443 to it are dropped.

### 17.5 Updates

- CI on `main` (the `release` job, environment `production`, the owner approves) builds `ghcr.io/mwijkhuisen/mjk-server`, `mjk-web` and `mjk-backup` with one revision label, pushes `:main` and `:sha-<git>` (public, no secrets inside) and records the digests; its `concurrency` group queues runs without cancelling them.
- `mjk-update.timer` (every 10 min) runs `deploy/bin/mjk-update`:
  1. take the deploy lock exclusive, without waiting; refuse (exit 1) when `table inet mjk` is not loaded (checked again, with the fail-closed marker, before each compose step)
  2. pull `:main`; wait (exit 0) while the three images' `org.opencontainers.image.revision` labels differ; stop if the digests and the compose file's sha256 equal `current.env`; stop with a loud log line if the set equals `failed.env` (`rm /srv/mjk/state/failed.env` to retry). A changed compose file alone is deployed with the same images through the same steps (P1e review)
  3. run `migrate`
  4. `up -d`
  5. smoke-test `http://127.0.0.1:8280/api/v1/healthz` and one museum page, both with `Host: <domain>` (A§17.2 answers any other `Host` with 421), then require every `mjk` container healthy
  6. on failure, write the set to `failed.env` and roll back to the digests in `/srv/mjk/state/current.env` (the compose file cannot be rolled back: fix it and run `mjk-install` again)
  7. after a success, remove the local references of `ghcr.io/mwijkhuisen/mjk-*` that `current.env` does not use (never another project's image; the disk is shared)
- Every docker step is bounded (`timeout`), and `TimeoutStartSec` is above their sum; a failed compose step logs the last lines of compose's error output.
- **A migration must work with the previous image** (expand now, contract in a later release): a rollback restores the old images but never runs `down`. The PR template asks for it.
- `current.env` and `failed.env` each hold the three refs `MJK_SERVER_IMAGE`, `MJK_WEB_IMAGE` and `MJK_BACKUP_IMAGE`, plus `MJK_COMPOSE_SHA256`; compose reads them with `--env-file` and requires each ref with `${VAR:?}`. One deploy lock, `/run/mjk/deploy.lock` (a root-only directory made at boot by `/etc/tmpfiles.d/mjk.conf`, never the world-writable `/run/lock`, where any local user could hold it; `mjk-install` never touches `/run/lock`; P1e review S2, S3): `mjk-update` takes it exclusive, `mjk-backup.service` and `mjk-tick` shared, so `mjk-update` skips with exit 0 while a backup or a tick holds it, and a long backup never stops the tick (P1e review).
- `mjk-update --server <ref> --web <ref> [--backup <ref>]` deploys given images (drills, CI); a ref with a registry host is recorded by digest, so `current.env` never names a movable tag.
- Compose, Caddyfile, nftables and units change rarely: the owner applies them with `deploy/bin/mjk-install` from a checkout on the VM. It is idempotent, leaves `rws` and `f1` untouched, and does not enable the timers until the secrets and `mjk.env` are filled (`docs/runbooks/install.md`). ### 17.6 Backups and restore

- **Nightly** at 01:43 Europe/Amsterdam (`mjk-backup.timer`, `Persistent=true`; clear of the Tuesday 03:17 sync and the 10-minute timers; owner, P1e Q3) `mjk-backup.service` runs, under the shared deploy lock, the `backup` role (ADR-0011, D24): `pg_dump -Fc --exclude-table-data` for `session`, `email_code`, `auth_challenge`, `rate_limit` and `job` (a restore signs everyone out) as `mjk_backup` into `/srv/mjk/backup-staging`, then restic 0.19.1 of the dump and `/srv/mjk/originals` to the private R2 EU bucket `mjk-backup`, then the local dump is deleted (in a `finally`) and the unit pings healthchecks. Details proven in P1e: a 0600 pgpass in a fresh 0700 directory on the tmpfs; `pg_dump --format=custom --lock-wait-timeout=60s`; `restic --no-cache backup --host mjk --tag nightly`; children get a fixed environment (never `process.env`), with `RESTIC_PASSWORD_FILE` and `AWS_SHARED_CREDENTIALS_FILE` pointing into `/run/secrets` (`r2_backup_put` is an AWS credentials file: `[default]` with `aws_access_key_id` and `aws_secret_access_key`); every run starts with `restic cat config`, and a missing repository (exit 10) is exit 78 `repo_missing`: the job never runs `restic init`, the owner creates the repository once from the workstation (`docs/runbooks/backup.md` §3; P1e security review S5); the endpoint must be https (plain http only on loopback, the MinIO stand-in) and the region defaults to `auto` (`MJK_R2_REGION`). Exit 0 · 69 database or repository unavailable · 70 failed, aborted or incomplete · 78 config (a wrong restic password is restic's exit 12). `pg_dump` accepts `--exclude-table-data` for tables that do not exist, and a restored dump holds the excluded tables' structure without their data. `pg_dump` 18.6 runs on distroless `cc-debian13` with its 12 libraries copied from `postgres:18.6-trixie`, so no Debian-slim stage is needed. restic 0.19.1 reads `AWS_SHARED_CREDENTIALS_FILE`. restic's backups, a refused `forget` and `locks/` under R2's lock rule are proven on the VM (below); still [U box]: the prune under the `index/` rule (after 2026-12-24) and the 256 MiB limit with a large originals set. The repository was created from the workstation on 2026-11-26 (`cef0632e`); the first two runs took 10 s each for a 0.6 MB dump and pinged healthchecks.
- **Lock:** bucket lock rules on `mjk-backup` keep objects under `data/`, `index/` and `snapshots/` for 28 days [U], and `keys/` and `config` **indefinitely** (P1e security review S1: restic writes them once, at `init`, so a 28-day age lock would let the VM's key delete them from day 29 and make every snapshot undecryptable). The owner keeps an offline copy of `config` and every `keys/*` object in the password manager; a key can be added but never removed, so the VM's key can also plant objects under `keys/` that stay for good. restic 0.19.1 (from its source: `internal/repository/key.go`, `internal/global/global.go`) tries at most 20 key files, and a key file that does not parse ends the search, so planted files could stop the repository from opening; the key it tries first is `--key-hint` (`RESTIC_KEY_HINT`), which the restore passes with the real key id (`docs/runbooks/backup.md` §1, §4; not tried against planted files [U]); a leaked restic password means a new repository (`docs/runbooks/secrets-rotation.md` §4). `locks/` is left out so restic can remove its own lock files. If `forget --prune` fails on recent `index/` files, `index/` comes out of the lock rule (restic can rebuild it); that fallback is recorded here and in ADR-0011's Revisit line. R2 has no put-only permission and no object versioning [U box, same check], so `r2_backup_put` is an Object Read & Write token scoped to `mjk-backup` only, and the lock rule is the only thing that stops it deleting recent history. Lock rules change only with an Admin token kept in the owner's password manager, never on the VM. Proven on the VM (2026-11-26, `docs/runbooks/backup.md` §5, canaries named `…-1795718050`): with the VM's key a new object under `data/` and `keys/` and a `config-…` object are written (200); an overwrite is refused under `data/` (409), and a delete is refused under `data/`, `keys/` and `config` (409); an overwrite under `keys/` and of `config` was not tried [U box: the day-28 check, `docs/runbooks/backup.md` §5 a]; a `forget` of a real snapshot fails with `The object is locked by the bucket policy` (restic retries for up to 15 minutes) and both snapshots stay; `locks/` takes a PUT and a DELETE (200, 204), and two backups in a row left no lock file; the key gets 403 on `mjk-media`. curl's `--aws-sigv4` signs for R2. The `config` and `keys/` canaries are checked again after day 28 (after 2026-12-24 18:34Z), and the prune under the `index/` rule then too (ADR-0011, Revisit).
- **Retention:** `restic forget --keep-daily 28 --prune --max-unused 0` [U box: the cost, measured in `docs/runbooks/backup.md` §6; on a young repository `forget` keeps every snapshot, so no prune runs before the first snapshots are older than 28 days], weekly, from the owner's workstation with its own token (Object R/W on `mjk-backup`, never on the VM). No weekly or monthly snapshots, so a deleted account leaves the backups within ~35 days (A§13.2) [U].
- **Restore drill**, monthly (`docs/runbooks/restore-drill.md`): restore the latest snapshot into a throw-away database, compare row counts per table with production, restore a sample of originals and check their sha256, then re-apply the account deletions since the snapshot: from production's `audit_log` when it survives; for a full loss, from the owner's deletion notices (the deletion job mails the owner "<account id> deleted <time>", ids only) [U]. The restore runbook keeps the site in maintenance until those deletions are re-run. The owner reads the result; the status panel shows the last drill. The first drill (P1e, 2026-11-26, snapshot `0040d024`): the dump restored into PostgreSQL 18.6 and every table's count equalled production; restore to counts took 9 s. Production's `job` was empty too, so only `backup.int.test.ts` shows its data left out until a drill meets a filled `job`.
- **Erasing one file from the backup** (owner, 2026-11-22; `docs/runbooks/backup-erasure.md`, P3c): for CSAM, a court order or another removal that cannot wait 35 days. A lock rule is no WORM: the Admin token in the owner's password manager can remove it (Cloudflare's bucket-lock docs, 2026-11-22). The runbook, from the owner's workstation: check the file is gone from `/srv/mjk/originals`; lift the rules on `data/`, `index/` and `snapshots/` (never `keys/` or `config`); `restic rewrite --exclude <path> --forget` on every snapshot that holds it, then `restic prune`; prove with `restic find` that no snapshot holds it; put the rules back and compare them with the list above. While the rules are off the VM's key could delete history, so the runbook keeps that window short; it also stops `mjk-backup.timer` for it, so no backup run meets the prune's exclusive lock.
- **RPO** ≤ 24 h for the database and the originals. **RTO** about a day [U]: reinstall, restore, rebuild the derivatives.
- **Not backed up:** the R2 derivatives (a worker job rebuilds them from the originals [U P3b]), the basemap tiles (re-extracted), the quarantine, export bundles, `/srv/mjk/tiles-build`, `/srv/mjk/state` (rebuilt by `mjk-install` and `mjk-update`), the images (in GHCR).
- The secrets are not in the backup: the owner keeps the restic password, the workstation token, the Admin token for lock rules and the R2 keys in a password manager off the VM; without them a restore is impossible.
- Accepted risk (D24): media and backups with one vendor in one account. The originals on the VM survive losing that account; the backups survive losing the VM.

The edge answers `api.lettermint.co` (proxied to the sink) and `test-team.cloudflareaccess.com` (the JWKS of `access-stub.mjs`, a key pair that lives for one run) under a throw-away CA that only `api` and `worker` trust (`NODE_EXTRA_CA_CERTS`): the production code dials its real URLs, with no test switch. `seed-e2e.ts` makes one owner per engine (a new code voids the last one, and the engines run in parallel), and `stack.sh` makes the invite and gives the roles (`admin` and `moderator`) through the production `admin` CLI. The admin spec sends its engine's Access JWT itself; the edge never adds one, so a request without it is the 401.
  - After the main pass, `stack.sh` checks that no email, password, invite, JWT signature or mailed code is in the `api`, `worker`, `ssr`, `caddy`, `db` or `mailsink` logs (the codes are read back from the sink, and an empty sink fails the check). The `@load` pass (Chromium only) sends 50 parallel password sign-ins, each answered 401 or 429, then checks the api's restart count, `OOMKilled` and `memory.events` (`oom_kill 0`) under its 384 MB limit, and the logs again.
  - The edge stands in for Cloudflare: TLS for `https://mjk.test` (the reserved `.test` TLD) with `tls internal`. Playwright joins the stack network with `ignoreHTTPSErrors`.
  - `cookie.spec.ts` proves that a `__Host-` cookie set by a response with `Secure` and `HttpOnly` round-trips in both engines, which is P2a's session cookie.
  - A second pass with `ssr` stopped checks the 502.
  - **The edge's certificate and later phases** (a probe on 2026-11-22 with Playwright 1.63 and Chromium 157, outside this project's pins): on a certificate the browser does not trust, with `ignoreHTTPSErrors` alone, a passkey from the virtual authenticator registers (P2b), but a service worker does not ("An SSL certificate error occurred when fetching the script"); with Chromium's `--ignore-certificate-errors` it does. P5a's service-worker and outbox specs therefore need the edge's CA trusted inside the Playwright container, or that launch argument; WebKit is [U P5a].
  - **Passkeys** (proven in P2b, 2026-11-27, Playwright 1.64.1 and its Chromium): with `ignoreHTTPSErrors` alone, over the edge's untrusted `tls internal` certificate, a CDP virtual authenticator (`ctap2`, internal, resident key, UV, automatic presence) registers and signs in for RP ID `mjk.test`; no `--ignore-certificate-errors-spki-list` is needed. With automatic presence on, the autofill (conditional) request the sign-in page arms completes by itself, before any click, so `passkeys.spec.ts` accepts either path and logs which ran (the autofill, in every run so far); the modal button is exercised by the refused sign-in after the removal. A removed passkey stays in the authenticator, the browser keeps offering it and the server answers 401 (the WebAuthn Signal API, `signalUnknownCredential`, could tell it to drop it: a follow-up). Playwright 1.64.1's WebKit has no `PublicKeyCredential`, so there the passkey sections do not render and criterion 7 covers the sign-in and account screens without passkey code; Safari itself is the owner's check (`docs/runbooks/accounts.md` B5).
  - One deviation from "0 `securitypolicyviolation` events" is accepted (owner, 2026-11-21, PR #41): WebKit's own report for each `<select>` (A§15.6). The guard in `apps/web/e2e/fixtures.ts` skips only the first report per `<select>` element, in WebKit only, so a `style=` of ours still fails in both engines.
- **Deploy** (job `ci / deploy`, from P1e): `deploy/tests/hardening.sh` (on `compose config`: the hardening anchor, UID 63532, limits, only 8280 published, no `rws` or `f1` names, subnets, GIDs or volumes, no `DATABASE_URL`), `nft-check.sh` (`nft -c` and a double load in a container, no `flush ruleset`) and `e2e.sh` (install, deploy, egress per network, rollback, `mjk-tick` reloading a deleted table).
- **Never in CI:** Wikidata, RCE, Lettermint, R2, Cloudflare, real phones. Those are [box] and [owner] criteria (PHASES §2).

## 20. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| The official app shows visit history again (the Mijn Museumkaart FAQ promises it) and the passport half loses its edge | What the official side lacks: reviews by card holders tied to visits, public profiles, the web and Android, better filters, € saved (ADR-0001). If the official history becomes exportable, members upload it (never scraped) |
| Cold start: too few reviews to matter | Hand-seeded beta (A11); a rating needs no text; a review prompt right after logging a visit (P3a); bulk onboarding; importing a member's own earlier reviews with their visits (A§10); the P4 gate measures reviews per member and museums with a review |
| Trademark: "Museumkaart" is protected; a shortlisted brand collides (museumlog.app) | Neutral brand in config and messages (ADR-0006, D23); BOIP and EUIPO check by hand before registering (A1); the public repository renamed to `mjk` (A1); descriptive use only and the "not affiliated" footer; a rename touches config and messages, not code |
| Museumkaart list accuracy and data access: no feed, public counts disagree (426–539), museum.nl text and lists are off limits | The curated flag with a published list version and date (A§7.5); "Meld een wijziging"; A5 asks for a feed or permission; Wikidata and RCE only (ADR-0005) |
| Illegal content and the moderation load on one person | Invite-only beta, the photo queue, text live but reportable, the hash blocklist, the CSAM tool; the P4 gate measures hours per week before open sign-up; the moderator role for help |
| Legal uncertainty: DSA applicability, trader status, IP retention, the database right, liability for photographed artworks; and no paid advice (owner, 2026-11-22) | ACM's guidance puts a site with no profit motive outside the DSA, and the hosting duties are built anyway (A§13.1); no revenue ever (D14); the owner's own assessment of every [U A9] item, written down in `docs/legal/assessment.md` before P3d's sign-off, the database-right item before A6; what stays open is an accepted risk with its name on it, and a claim, a regulator's letter or any revenue idea reopens the question of paid advice |
| The Wikidata Query Service changes engine: Blazegraph may degrade from February 2027 and is switched off on 2027-06-30 (A§7.1) | One dated maintenance PR before February 2027 (PHASES §1, A13): the new endpoint, the query re-checked without its optimizer hint, fixtures recorded again; until then a failed run ends `wikidata_unavailable`, retries and changes nothing |
| Cloudflare's terms for media and tiles change, or R2's custom domain on an EU bucket misbehaves [U] | Media and tiles on R2, nothing large through the tunnel (ADR-0008, ADR-0014); P1d and P3b prove the custom domain; derivatives and tiles can be rebuilt, so a move is a rebuild, a re-upload and an ADR |
| One vendor for media and backups (D24) | Accepted; the originals also live on the VM; a locked bucket and per-bucket tokens, no account-wide token on the VM; hardware-key 2FA on the account [U] |
| Home line, power, one disk | Accepted for a hobby site (ADR-0003); RPO ≤ 24 h; the reinstall and restore runbooks; the edge cache serves public pages for up to the `s-maxage` only |
| Resource contention with Waterheight and F1 | Limits per container (A§17.2), sharp one job at a time, the VM resize (A3), measurements after P1e, P4 and P5b; video only after a measured trial (P7) |
| Dependency churn and the 7-day rule (2026 versions newer than most model knowledge) | Few dependencies; exact pins; the CLAUDE.md gotchas; Dependabot cooldown; own code where a library churns (better-auth, aws4fetch) |
| Security bugs in our own auth code | A small surface (codes, an optional password, passkeys through `@simplewebauthn`), Lucia's documented approach, the A§16.2 table as the review checklist, `review:security` on P2a and P2b, tests per criterion; admin also behind Access (D22) |
| Fast-moving small competitors (Cultra; MuseumsAtlas with its own "Museumpaspoort") | Ship the passport-first beta and own the public, web-first, review half |
| Fake, rewarded or staff reviews, and defamation claims | Terms forbid them; one review per member per museum, tied to a visit; notice and action; the email and the 30-day IP log kept for court orders (A§13.4) |
